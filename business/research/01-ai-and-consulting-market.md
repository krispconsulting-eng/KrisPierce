# Research notes: AI capability and patient engagement consulting markets

Compiled 15 November 2026 from web search summaries. Page fetches were blocked in this session, so every figure comes from search-result summaries, not the primary documents. Check the primary source before quoting a figure publicly. Prices are vendor list prices, not market averages.

## Demand for AI capability

- Infoxchange, Digital Technology in the Not-for-Profit Sector 2025 (824 organisations, Australia and New Zealand): AI adoption roughly doubled in a year; only 14% have an AI policy or governance framework. https://www.infoxchange.org/digital-technology-not-for-profit-sector
- PwC Australia (May 2026) citing the same report: 67% use generative AI, 14% have an AI policy or guideline. https://www.pwc.com.au/media/2026/helping-not-for-profits-thrive-in-the-age-of-ai.html
- 23% have a documented cyber security plan. https://itbrief.com.au/story/ai-use-in-not-for-profits-surges-but-digital-gaps-remain-stark
- Infoxchange received Commonwealth funding through the National AI Centre (December 2025) to build safe AI capability in the sector: a demand signal and a subsidised competitor. https://www.infoxchange.org/news/2025/12/new-national-ai-initiative-strengthen-ai-safety-and-capability-across-sector
- Journal of Participatory Medicine 2026, 115 rare disease patients and caregivers (US recruitment): 54.8% had used generative AI in a rare disease context; 78.3% had not discussed AI-generated information with a clinician. https://jopm.jmir.org/2026/1/e93720
- European Patients' Forum AI Survey Report 2026 (874 responses): 82% want to be told when a clinician uses AI; identifies an AI literacy need. https://www.eu-patient.eu/news/latest-epf-news/2026/ai-survey-report-2026/
- Not found: any AI programme from EURORDIS, Global Genes, NORD or Rare Voices Australia; budget data.

## What this means

1. The gap between AI use (67%) and AI policy (14%) is the clearest opening. Safe-use policy and governance work is a more defensible first offer than general AI training.
2. General not-for-profit AI training is free or cheap. A paid offer has to be specific to rare disease and health data.
3. Small charities pay about USD 1,500 to 4,500 for fixed-fee AI readiness work. Pharma teams are a separate, higher-budget buyer.
4. The July 2026 consumer engagement framework, the PBAC Guidelines update and the 2027 Strategic Agreement renewal give sponsors and patient organisations a timed reason to build consumer evidence capability. IQVIA is already positioning there.
5. Consulting should be priced as professional services, well above consumer sitting-fee schedules, with Code Edition 20 transparency built into contracts.
