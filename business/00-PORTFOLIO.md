# Portfolio: which businesses to build, and in what order

Prepared 15 November 2026 for Kris Pierce. Read `01-GUARDRAILS.md` alongside this. Evidence sits in `research/`. Decisions only Kris can make are in `DECISIONS-FOR-KRIS.md`.

## The answer

Build one business with three product lines, launched in sequence, sharing one audience and one back office.

1. **Kris Pierce Consulting: engagement advisory, fronted by the CEOF Diagnostic.** Sell now. It earns from the first month and funds the other two.
2. **Rare Intelligence: AI capability for the people who run rare disease organisations.** Founding members from December 2026, public launch on Rare Disease Day, 28 February 2027. This is the line that can grow without selling more of Kris's days.
3. **Caregiver Wellbeing: sponsor-funded places on the eight-week Guided Journey.** Sponsor conversations from March 2027, first funded cohort mid-2027, results at Carers Week in October 2027. Slowest to revenue, largest reach, and the asset most likely to be licensed.

Rare and Raw is the shared marketing channel, not a profit centre. The Lived Experience Network licence, the navigation pathway and convening are parked, for reasons of ownership more than merit.

## Why not everything

The handover lists eight ventures. Kris also holds a UNSW role, a charity directorship and three public appointments. The binding constraint is days, not ideas. I have assumed about two days a week for commercial work, roughly 80 to 90 days a year (Kris to confirm). Eight ventures on 85 days is eight half-built things. Three lines that feed each other is a business.

## How the ventures ranked

Scored 1 to 5. Revenue is 12-month potential for a solo operator. Effort is how close it is to sellable (5 is ready). Conflict is how cleanly it sits beside Kris's other roles (5 is clean).

| Venture | Revenue | Effort | Conflict | Total | Call |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| KPC engagement advisory with CEOF Diagnostic | 5 | 5 | 3 | 13 | Build first |
| Rare Intelligence | 4 | 4 | 4 | 12 | Build second |
| Caregiver Wellbeing | 3 | 3 | 4 | 10 | Build third |
| Rare and Raw podcast | 1 | 3 | 4 | 8 | Marketing channel for all three |
| Lived Experience Network licence | 3 | 1 | 2 | 6 | Park until the UNSW IP check is done |
| AI evaluation and rubric work | 2 | 3 | 4 | 9 | Fold into Rare Intelligence as a service and a content theme |
| Care navigation pathway | 2 | 2 | 1 | 5 | SCN2A Australia's asset; board decision |
| Events and convening | 2 | 2 | 1 | 5 | The live proposals belong to UNSW and SCN2A Australia |

Reasons, briefly:

- **Consulting scores highest on revenue and readiness** because the site, the services and the reputation exist. It has no prices and one call to action, which is why it under-earns. Its conflict score is 3 because one live page offers sponsors submission-specific HTA advice while Kris sits on HTA bodies. Fix that and it is a 4.
- **Rare Intelligence has a built site and a full course catalogue**, and the market gap is specific: 67% of Australian not-for-profits use generative AI and 14% have a policy for it. General AI training is free elsewhere, so the offer must stay narrowly rare disease.
- **Caregiver Wellbeing has the strongest need** (61.1% of carers report low wellbeing against 33.6% of adults) and locked branding, but the content is unapproved, the Wheel is not rebuilt and the sponsor position is open. It cannot be sold until Kris approves a first version.
- **The podcast cannot earn on downloads.** A top-tenth podcast gets about 400 downloads an episode; at normal sponsorship rates that is $12. A flat season sponsorship of a few thousand dollars is possible later.
- **The Lived Experience Network licence** has one line of documentation, and the working example (Rare Power) is owned outright by Rare Diseases NSW. It could be valuable in 2028. It is not a 2027 product.

## How the three lines feed each other

The same buyer often needs all three. A patient organisation chief executive takes the free CEOF Diagnostic, books a debrief, joins the Signal Circle to get the team using AI safely, and asks a funder to sponsor caregiver places for the families they serve. A pharma patient engagement lead commissions an advisory sprint, then funds Signal Circle places and a caregiver cohort for patient organisations as unrestricted, disclosed support.

## Twelve-month revenue model (calendar 2027)

Built from price times volume. Not drawn from Kris's accounts, which I did not open. All figures AUD, excluding GST. Prices are my recommendations.

| Line | Conservative | Base | Stretch |
|---|---|---|---|
| KPC advisory | 62,000 | 137,000 | 215,000 |
| Rare Intelligence | 28,000 | 79,000 | 142,000 |
| Caregiver Wellbeing | 7,500 | 39,000 | 84,000 |
| **Total** | **97,500** | **255,000** | **441,000** |
| Delivery days needed (approx.) | 45 | 100 | 150 |

Base case detail:

- KPC: 8 diagnostic debriefs (12,000); 3 advisory sprints (36,000); 2 retainers at $3,500 for 10 months (70,000); 2 in-house co-design training days (19,000).
- Rare Intelligence: membership ramping to 50 members, about 300 member-months at a blended $82 (24,600); 8 readiness consults (12,000); 4 policy sprints (14,000); 2 organisational intensives (16,000); 2 workshops of 12 people at $520 (12,480).
- Caregiver Wellbeing: 2 pilot cohorts at $7,500 (15,000); 1 full cohort (12,000); 1 organisation licence (12,000).

Two things to notice. The base case needs about 100 days, more than the 85 I assumed, so either retainers are capped at two or a contractor takes on community management and cohort administration by mid-year. And the membership is the only line where revenue grows without more days, which is the argument for protecting it even while it is small.

Start-up cost is low: trade marks (about $250 a class), Skool (about US$99 a month), email platform, a lawyer's review of three agreements and the privacy policy, a bookkeeper. Allow $6,000 to $9,000 for the year.
