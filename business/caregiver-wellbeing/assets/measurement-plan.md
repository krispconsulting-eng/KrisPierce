Where it goes: internal. Once decided, it feeds the consent wording, the Wheel rebuild, the intake and week-eight workflows, and the impact report template.

Decisions this works from (DECISIONS.md, 10 October 2026): eight equally weighted Wheel domains; results are reflections, never scores; no points, streaks or badges; no invented people or outcomes. # Measurement plan: proposal

## 1. What the measurement is for

Two purposes only:

1. To let a caregiver see their own reflection at the start and at week eight.
2. To tell a sponsor, honestly and in aggregate, what changed for the group.

Anything that serves neither purpose is not collected.

## 2. What to measure

| What | Week one | Week eight | Seen by the caregiver | Seen by the sponsor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Wheel reflections, eight areas | Yes | Yes | Yes, as their own reflection | Group patterns only |
| A short wellbeing measure | Yes | Yes | No score is shown | Group average and change only |
| Completion | Automatic | Automatic | No | Counts only |
| Two experience questions | No | Yes | Their own answers | Group counts only |

### 2.1 The Wheel

The Wheel is the heart of the programme and is the caregiver's own. It is completed at week one and again at week eight. For measurement, the answers are used only as group patterns for each of the eight areas. See section 6.

The answer format depends on the rebuilt Wheel, which is an open decision. Recommendation already in BRAIN.md: 24 questions for launch.

### 2.2 A short validated wellbeing measure

Recommended: the Personal Wellbeing Index, adult version, subject to confirming its licence in writing.

Why:

- It is short. That fits the low-burden promise.
- It is understood to be free to use with acknowledgement (unverified in the research notes).
- It is understood to have Australian norms (unverified), which gives a sponsor a reference point.
- It sits in the same measure family as the national Carer Wellbeing Survey, according to the research notes, so results can be read beside the 61.1% figure. This needs checking against the survey report.
- It asks about satisfaction with areas of life. It is not a symptom checklist and does not read as clinical.

What must be confirmed before use:

- The licence terms for use by a business, in writing.
- The exact items, wording, answer scale and scoring rules, from the official manual. Do not reword any item.
- The required acknowledgement.
- Whether the Australian norms can be quoted in a sponsor report.

Fallback if the licence does not allow this use: WHO-5, which is understood to be free (unverified). It would also need its terms confirmed.

Not recommended:

| Measure | Why not |
|---|---|
| ASCOT-Carer | A licence is required, and for-profit use is costed case by case (University of Kent licensing page, per the research notes). Cost and timing are unknown. |
| Zarit Burden Interview | Licensed through Mapi Research Trust, with commercial terms on quote (per the research notes). It also measures burden, which frames caring as a weight. That sits badly with the brand voice. |

### 2.3 Completion

Recorded automatically, with no extra effort from the caregiver:

- Enrolled.
- Completed the first Wheel.
- Opened or acted on a weekly message [depends on what the platform can record without tracking pixels; see section 4].
- Completed the second Wheel.

Completion is reported for the group. It is never shown to the caregiver as progress, a streak or a percentage.

### 2.4 Two plain-language experience questions

Asked once, at week eight. Proposed wording for Kris to edit:

1. "How did the eight weeks fit into your life?"
   - It fitted easily
   - It fitted some weeks
   - It was hard to fit in

2. "Did one small thing a week feel useful to you?"
   - Yes
   - A little
   - Not really

One optional open box: "Anything you want us to know?" Answers to the open box are read by Kris only and are never quoted without written consent for the exact wording.

## 3. A point for Kris to decide first

The Wheel gives reflections, never scores. A validated wellbeing measure does produce a number for each person.

The proposal keeps these apart:

- The caregiver is never shown a number, a band or a label from the wellbeing measure.
- The number is used only inside a group average.
- The consent wording tells the caregiver plainly that these few questions are for the group report.

If Kris judges that any per-person number is out of keeping with the programme, the alternative is to report the Wheel and the two experience questions only. The cost is that sponsors receive no validated measure, and the results cannot be read beside national data.

## 4. What not to measure

- No diagnosis, condition or medication, for the caregiver or the person they care for.
- No depression, anxiety or distress screening tools. This is a non-clinical programme and must not screen.
- No name or details of the person being cared for.
- No date of birth, address or full postcode.
- No employer, team, site or job title, even in an employer cohort.
- No income.
- No device, location or behaviour tracking beyond what the platform needs to work.
- No time-on-page, streaks, counts of actions per person or any ranking of caregivers.
- No follow-up measures after week eight in the pilot, unless Kris adds a consented follow-up later.

## 5. Data minimisation

- Collect a first name or chosen name, an email address, and the answers above. Nothing else.
- Give each caregiver a private code at enrolment. Store answers under the code, separate from the name and email.
- Remove names and email addresses before any analysis.
- Hold data in Australia where practical [storage location to be confirmed].
- Limit access to Kris and [named person, if any].
- Keep identifiable information for [period to be decided] after the cohort ends, then delete it. Keep de-identified group results.
- Delete a person's information on request, and say how long that takes [period to be decided].
- Never pass anything identifiable to a sponsor or employer.

## 6. Analysis approach

### The Wheel, without individual scores

- For each of the eight areas, count how many caregivers chose each answer option at week one and at week eight.
- Report those counts side by side, with one plain sentence for each area.
- Do not add a person's answers into a total. Do not average a person's answers across areas. Do not rank areas for a person or for the group.
- Do not label anyone, or any area, as low, high, at risk or improved.
- Count which area caregivers chose to focus on, if the journey asks that.
- Merge or hide any option chosen by fewer than ten people.

### The wellbeing measure

- Score it exactly as its manual says.
- Report the group average and spread at week one and week eight, for people who answered at both points.
- Report the change in the group average, with a 95% interval, in plain words.
- Show the published Australian reference range beside it if the licence allows.
- No tests across subgroups. The groups are too small.

### Completion and drop-out

- Report every stage from enrolled to completed.
- Compare week-one group averages for people who finished and people who did not, where both groups have ten or more people.
- State plainly that results describe people who finished.

### Minimum group size

No figure for fewer than ten people. If fewer than ten complete both points, report participation only.

## 7. The limits of what a pilot can show

A pilot of up to 50 caregivers can show:

- Whether caregivers enrol, and how many reach week eight.
- Whether the Wheel and the weekly message are easy to use.
- How the group's reflections and group wellbeing average differ between week one and week eight.
- What caregivers say about fit and usefulness.

It cannot show:

- That the programme caused any change. There is no comparison group.
- That the change lasts. There is no follow-up.
- That results would be the same for other caregivers. People chose to take part.
- Anything about any single person.
- Anything clinical.

The honest claim after a pilot is "informed by evidence, with early results from [n] caregivers". Results are written with their limits beside them, every time.

## 8. What Kris needs to decide

1. Whether to use a validated measure at all, given section 3.
2. Personal Wellbeing Index or WHO-5, once licences are confirmed in writing.
3. The exact wording of the two experience questions.
4. The Wheel depth and answer format, which sets how section 6 works.
5. How long identifiable information is kept.
6. Who, other than Kris, can see the data.
7. Whether an independent person checks the analysis before a report is published.
8. Whether a pilot needs ethics review. It is not needed for service evaluation in many settings, but publishing results may change that. Ask the lawyer or a university contact.
