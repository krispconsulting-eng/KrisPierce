What this is: short support wording, in three lengths, for any screen or email where a caregiver may be struggling.
Where it goes: the Wheel start and result screens, every weekly email footer, the week-eight screens, and the Wellbeing page. For Kris's approval before use.

Notes:

- Phone numbers are from BRAIN.md. Check each number and its opening hours before publishing. Hours are not stated here because they were not confirmed.
- Use the wording exactly. Do not add urgency, capital letters or warning colours.
- On screens, the numbers should be tappable links.

---

# One line

Use in email footers and under buttons.

> Need support now? Carer Gateway 1800 422 737. Lifeline 13 11 14. In an emergency, call 000.

Where health is discussed, add:

> Common experiences, not a diagnosis. If you're worried, talk to your GP.

---

# Short block

Use at the end of the Wheel, and on any screen that asks about feelings, energy or money.

> ## If things feel heavy
>
> You do not have to sort it out alone.
>
> - Carer Gateway: 1800 422 737. Free support for caregivers.
> - Lifeline: 13 11 14. Someone to talk to, any time.
> - In an emergency, call 000.
>
> Common experiences, not a diagnosis. If you're worried, talk to your GP.

---

# Full block

Use on a "Support" page linked from every screen, and in the first email after enrolment.

> ## Support, if you need it
>
> Caregiver Wellbeing is a place to pause and look at your own life. It is not health care, counselling or a crisis service. If you need more than this, these services are there for you.
>
> ### If you or someone else is in danger
>
> Call 000.
>
> ### If you need to talk to someone now
>
> Lifeline: 13 11 14. You can call at any time of the day or night.
>
> ### If you want support as a caregiver
>
> Carer Gateway: 1800 422 737. This is the Australian Government's free service for caregivers. You can ask about counselling, coaching, respite and practical help.
>
> ### If you are worried about your health
>
> Talk to your GP. You can tell them you are a caregiver. It helps them understand what your days are like.
>
> ### A note on what you see here
>
> What you read and reflect on here describes common experiences. It is not a diagnosis. If you're worried, talk to your GP.
>
> Questions about the programme: info@krispierce.com.au. We read email on weekdays and cannot respond to urgent needs.
