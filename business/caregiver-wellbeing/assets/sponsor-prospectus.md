## Three million people, and most are not doing well

- About 3.0 million Australians are unpaid caregivers. That is almost 12% of people living in households. About 1.2 million are primary caregivers, up from 861,600 in 2018. Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics, Survey of Disability, Ageing and Carers 2022.
- 61.1% of caregivers have low wellbeing, compared with 33.6% of Australian adults. The figure for caregivers was 57.7% a year earlier. Source: 2025 Carer Wellbeing Survey, University of Canberra for Carers Australia, 10,918 responses.
- Caring affects work. In 2018 the employment-to-population ratio was 55.8% for primary caregivers and 77.4% for people who were not caregivers. Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics, Survey of Disability, Ageing and Carers 2018.

## What is missing

Most support asks a caregiver to do more: attend, book, read, complete. People who are already stretched often cannot. Caregiver Wellbeing is built to ask for very little. One small thing a week, under five minutes.

# Page 2: what it is and how the eight weeks work

## What Caregiver Wellbeing is

Caregiver Wellbeing is a non-clinical wellbeing program for any unpaid caregiver, whether they care for a child, a young person, an adult or an ageing parent. It is online, private and self-paced. It was created by Kris Pierce, who has been a caregiver for 20 years.

It is not a health service, counselling or a crisis service. It does not diagnose, treat, prevent or screen for any condition. It sits beside free services such as Carer Gateway and points to them.

## How the eight weeks work

1. The Wheel. A caregiver starts with a short reflection on their own life across eight areas:
   - Body & energy
   - Feelings & emotions
   - People & connection
   - Purpose & everyday life
   - Money & practical needs
   - Home & surroundings
   - Learning & interests
   - Meaning & what matters

   The eight areas are equally weighted. The result is a reflection, never a score. Nothing is ranked.

2. One small thing a week. Each week for eight weeks, the caregiver receives a short message and chooses one small thing to do for themselves. It takes under five minutes. Skipping a week is fine. There are no points, streaks, badges or reminders that create pressure.

3. A second Wheel at week eight. The caregiver looks again at the same eight areas and sees, in their own words, what has shifted and what has not.

## What it is built on

The program is informed by evidence. Published meta-analyses of internet-based and digital programs for caregivers report short-term reductions in stress and burden (Sherifali and colleagues, Journal of Medical Internet Research, 2018; a 2025 meta-analysis in Frontiers in Digital Health). Many of the studies in those reviews carry a high risk of bias, and no trial has tested this exact format. The pilot cohorts are how the evidence for this program is built, and the results are reported honestly, including who dropped out.

# Page 4: what you receive, and how caregivers are invited

## What you receive

- An aggregate impact report for your cohort. It shows how many caregivers started, how many finished, what changed for the group, and what caregivers said about the experience in two short questions.
- Acknowledgement in plain words, for example: "Places in this cohort are funded by [organisation name]."
- For pilot sponsors, acknowledgement as a founding supporter when the pilot report is published.

## What you do not receive

- No names or contact details of caregivers.
- No individual results, answers or reflections.
- No figure for any group smaller than ten people.
- No say over the content, the caregivers who take part, or the findings.
- No product presence. No product names, promotion or messages reach caregivers.

These terms are written into the agreement. They apply to every sponsor, without exception.

## How caregivers are invited

- You share a private enrolment link through your own channels, such as a newsletter, an intranet page or a letter. We supply the wording.
- You do not send us a list of people. Caregivers choose for themselves and enrol directly with Caregiver Wellbeing.
- You are never told who enrolled.
- A caregiver can stop at any time, with no reason needed, and can ask for their information to be deleted.
- For community places, caregivers apply to an open pool through the Wellbeing page on krispierce.com.au.

## The impact report

You receive one report about four weeks after your cohort's window closes [timing to be confirmed]. It contains:

1. Participation: invited where known, enrolled, started, completed week eight.
2. The Wheel at week one and week eight, shown for the group across the eight areas, as reflections and never as scores.
3. Change on a short wellbeing measure, for the group [measure to be confirmed, see the measurement plan].
4. Two plain-language experience questions.
5. Who did not finish, and what that means for reading the results.
6. Limits and caveats, in plain words.

A pilot cohort has no comparison group. The report can describe what changed for the people who took part. It cannot show that the program caused the change. We say so in the report.
