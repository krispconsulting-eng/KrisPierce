# Diagnostic Debrief: product file

Internal. This is the whole product: what is sold, how it is delivered, and how long each step takes. Follow it as written so every client gets the same debrief.

Price: $1,500, excluding GST. Kris's time: 0.6 day (about 4 hours 50 minutes). Source of truth for price and time: `../../BRAIN.md`, section 4. Names of domains and levels: `../CEOF-REFERENCE.md`.

Public copy for this offer (results page block, report email paragraph, booking page) already exists in `../../assets/diagnostic-debrief-offer.md`. Do not rewrite it here. This folder holds the delivery kit.

## 1. The promise

In one 90-minute session and one written plan, the client goes from seven scores to three priorities, a list of what to stop, and a 90-day calendar with an owner against every action.

## 2. Who it is for

- An organisation that has completed the CEOF Diagnostic and wants to act on it.
- The person who booked can make, or directly influence, decisions about how the organisation involves its community.
- There is a decision or deadline behind the booking: a grant round, a board meeting, a funder report, a partnership, a new program.

Not a fit, and say so before taking the fee:

- No diagnostic completed. Send the diagnostic link first.
- The client wants a full strategy. That is a Strategic Advisory Sprint.
- The client wants help with a specific application before a committee Kris sits on or advises. Out of scope, always.

## 3. What is included

| Included | Detail |

## 4. What is excluded

- Interviews, document review beyond what the questionnaire asks for, or any work with community members. (No consumers take part in a debrief, so no consumer payments apply. If the plan recommends involving consumers, it says they are paid at published rates, costed as a separate line.)
- A second session, or revisions to the plan beyond correcting errors of fact.
- Product-specific work on any application that is, or is likely to be, before a committee Kris sits on or advises.
- Medical, legal or regulatory advice.

## 6. What the client prepares

- The CEOF Diagnostic, completed, using the same email address as the booking.
- The pre-session questionnaire, returned two working days before the session (ten minutes).
- Up to three colleagues who are involved in the decisions the plan will touch. At least one person in the session must be able to say yes to an owner and a date.
- Nothing else. No slides, no documents.

## 7. Quality bar

The plan is not sent until every line below is true.

- [ ] It names the decision or deadline it serves, in the cover block.
- [ ] Six pages or fewer. No appendices.
- [ ] The seven domain names and the level name match `../CEOF-REFERENCE.md` exactly.
- [ ] Each priority has all five parts: why it matters here, first move, 90-day action, owner, measure.
- [ ] Every owner is a named person or role that was agreed in the session, not assigned afterwards.
- [ ] Every measure can be checked by someone outside the organisation.
- [ ] "What to stop" contains at least one real activity the client agreed to stop or pause.
- [ ] The calendar has no more than nine actions, and one of them happens this week.
- [ ] The outside-help section says honestly whether a sprint is the right step. If it is not, it says so.
- [ ] No em dashes, no exclamation marks, no banned words, no "we".
- [ ] Nothing in it refers to Kris's public appointments or to any live application.
- [ ] Footer on every page.

## 8. How it leads to a sprint

The debrief is the paid step between the free diagnostic and a Strategic Advisory Sprint. It leads there only when the session turns up one decision that the client cannot settle alone.

- In the session (minute 80 to 87), Kris asks which of the three priorities the team can carry alone and which one hinges on a decision nobody has made yet.
- Page 6 of the plan names that one decision, if there is one, and says which of the four sprint types would serve it: a consumer evidence plan, an engagement strategy, a trial-readiness review or a partnership approach.
- The $1,500 fee is credited against a sprint booked within 60 days of the debrief. Record the debrief date and the day-60 date in the pipeline tracker.
- The 30-day check-in mentions the credit once, with the date it lapses. No further follow-up unless the client replies.
- If the honest answer is that the client needs no outside help, the plan says that. Target conversion is 1 in 3, not 3 in 3.

## 9. Risks and how each is handled

| Risk | Handling |
|---|---|
| The booker cannot commit owners or dates | Confirmation email asks for a decision-maker in the session. If none attends, the plan lists owners as "to be confirmed by [name] by [date]" and the first action is to confirm them. |
| The session becomes a general conversation | The session guide is timed. Kris states the three outputs in the first five minutes and returns to them. |
| The client disputes the scores | Scores are a starting line, not a verdict. Kris records the client's reading on page 1 under "what the session changed" and moves on. The priorities can be swapped using the rule in the session guide. |
| The plan grows past six pages or past 120 minutes | The template has fixed slots. Fill them, cut the rest. |
| The client asks for more (a second session, a document review) | Name the offer that covers it: a sprint or the retainer. Do not give it away inside the debrief. |
| A conflict appears in the session (a product heading to a committee Kris sits on or advises) | Stop that line of discussion, say why in one sentence, and keep the plan to general engagement capability. Record it. |
| The diagnostic was completed by one person guessing | Question 3 of the questionnaire asks who completed it. If one person, the 15 to 25 minute pass through the domains tests each score with the group. |
| Client names and results | Never used in public without written consent. Diagnostic data is used in benchmarks only in de-identified form and with consent. |
