# Diagnostic Debrief: question sets for a company

Internal. Use with `session-guide.md` when the client completed the Patient Engagement Review. The agenda, the priority rules, the stop questions and the ready lines are all in the session guide and are not repeated here. This file replaces section 3 of that guide for company sessions only.

Written for an Australian affiliate: decisions that are partly made overseas, four or five functions that each meet patients, a compliance team with a view, and patient organisations that deal with all of them.

## Before a company session

- The conflict check is done and recorded (`intake-and-conflict-check-form.md`).
- Say this in the first three minutes, in these words: "This session looks at how your company engages, as a capability. It does not assess any product, and I do not work on product-specific submissions or advise on how a committee is likely to respond."
- Ask the room not to name products or pipeline assets. "Your rare disease program" is enough.
- If a product, a submission or a committee comes up, use the line in the session guide, section 5, and move on.
- Everything agreed must sit inside the Medicines Australia Code of Conduct and the company's own policies. If compliance is not in the room, each action that touches payment, patient organisations or patient contact carries the note "subject to compliance review" and a name.

How to use each set: the check question for every domain in minutes 15 to 25; the four probing questions only for the three priorities; then land the five parts in the order shown. The first move is word for word from `../01-ceof-diagnostic/AUDIENCE-VERSIONS.md`. Adapt it with the client. Do not replace it.

## Domain 1. Purpose and Governance

Check question: "If a patient organisation asked who in this affiliate owns the relationship with them, would every function give the same name?"

Probing questions:

1. Where is your patient engagement strategy written down, and which decisions does it say engagement must inform? Is it a local document or a global one applied locally?
2. Who is accountable across the affiliate, and does that person have authority over what medical, market access, commercial and corporate affairs each do?
3. When did the leadership team last review engagement as its own agenda item, and whose budget pays for it?
4. Has a patient organisation ever been approached by two functions in the same month? What happened?

Landing the five parts:

| Part | How to land it |
|---|---|
| Why here | Tie it to a planning or budget date, or to a relationship with a patient organisation that is under strain. |
| First move | Write one page for the leadership team: what patient engagement is for here, who is accountable, and the three decisions this year it must inform. |
| 90-day action | The page adopted by the leadership team, a named owner, and one agreed contact route per patient organisation. |
| Owner | A leadership team member, not the patient engagement lead alone. If the general manager is absent, the owner is "to be confirmed by [name] by [date]". |
| Measure | A leadership team minute, and a contact list that a patient organisation could be shown. |

## Domain 2. Voice and Representation

Check question: "Outside advisory boards, how does a patient or carer get heard here, and who have you never heard from?"

Probing questions:

1. Who do you hear from now, and how many of them come through the same two or three patient organisations?
2. Where are carers, and people who belong to no organisation, in what you hear?
3. When trial design, an evidence plan or a support program is decided locally, is patient insight present at the meeting or summarised afterwards?
4. Name one time local patient input changed something decided by a global team. How long before the decision did it need to arrive?

Landing the five parts:

| Part | How to land it |
|---|---|
| Why here | A decision this year that will be made without patients unless something changes, or a global deadline local input keeps missing. |
| First move | Set up one standing patient and carer panel with terms of reference, and put one real decision in front of it this quarter. |
| 90-day action | Terms of reference approved, members recruited beyond the usual organisations, first meeting held on an open decision. Payment at fair market value, costed as a separate line. |
| Owner | The person who will chair or sponsor the panel, with compliance named as reviewer of the terms. |
| Measure | The terms, the membership by type (not by name), and the decision that went to the first meeting. |

## Domain 3. Capability and Culture

Check question: "What training does someone here get before they first meet a patient or a patient organisation, and how much of it is about what they must not do?"

Probing questions:

1. Which teams meet patients or patient organisations: medical, access, commercial, corporate affairs, clinical operations, patient support? Who has been left off that list?
2. Who briefs a patient or carer before an advisory board or a speaking role, and what do they receive?
3. When a plan comes to the leadership team, does anyone ask what patients said? Who?
4. Is engagement in the medical, access and brand plans at the start of the cycle, or added once budgets are set?

Landing the five parts:

| Part | How to land it |
|---|---|
| Why here | Staff who hold back for fear of the Code, or a patient who had a poor experience that reached a patient organisation. |
| First move | Run one session for every team that meets patients: engagement practice first, compliance second. |
| 90-day action | The session delivered to every listed team, a briefing pack for patients and carers in use, one line on engagement in plan sign-off. |
| Owner | The accountable leader, with compliance as co-presenter, so practice and rules are taught together. |
| Measure | Attendance by team, the briefing pack itself, and the sign-off template. |

If the client wants the session built and run for them, that is in-house training, not part of the debrief. Say so.

## Domain 4. Engagement Practice

Check question: "Think of your last patient activity. What decision was it for, and was that decision still open, here or globally?"

Probing questions:

1. At what stage of development does this affiliate first involve patients? Who decides that: local or global?
2. For your last three activities, who chose the method, and was it chosen before or after the question was clear?
3. When would your presence in the room shape what patients say? Who facilitates then?
4. What do you ask of participants: travel, time off work, care arrangements, reading? Who checked it was reasonable?

Landing the five parts:

| Part | How to land it |
|---|---|
| Why here | An activity planned for the next quarter whose decision has already been made, or a plan cycle about to start. |
| First move | Add one line to every plan template: what decision is this, and when did patients inform it? |
| 90-day action | The line in medical, access and brand plan templates, tested on one plan in each function, and one activity moved earlier as a result. |
| Owner | Whoever owns the planning templates, usually business operations or the function heads, named individually. |
| Measure | The templates, and the one activity with its old and new dates. |

Keep examples general. Do not discuss the design of any activity tied to a submission.

## Domain 5. Safety, Ethics and Recognition

Check question: "If two functions each engaged the same carer this month, would that person be paid the same rate, on the same terms, in the same time?"

Probing questions:

1. What does each function pay, on what basis is fair market value set, and how long do payment and reimbursement take?
2. Where are staff least sure of the line under the Medicines Australia Code of Conduct, and what do they do when unsure?
3. For each patient organisation you fund: is the support disclosed, is any of it tied to an activity you chose, and could they disagree with you in public without cost?
4. How are consent, privacy and adverse event reporting handled in each activity, and do participants know what happens to what they say?

Landing the five parts:

| Part | How to land it |
|---|---|
| Why here | A patient organisation's independence, the company's standing with it, and staff confidence to engage at all. |
| First move | Write down your payment and independence principles for patients and patient organisations, publish them, and apply them the same way in every function. |
| 90-day action | One policy agreed by compliance, finance and the functions; principles published; funded organisations told. |
| Owner | The accountable leader with the compliance lead. Both names go in the plan. |
| Measure | The published principles, and a payment-time figure the company is willing to state. |

I do not give legal or Code interpretation advice. The company's compliance team decides what the Code requires; the plan records who will ask them.

## Domain 6. Feedback and Learning

Check question: "The members of your last advisory board: do they know what changed because of them?"

Probing questions:

1. After an activity, who goes back to participants, with what, and how long does internal approval of that message take?
2. Where is patient insight kept, and can another function find it before asking the same people again?
3. When did you last ask a patient organisation what you are like to work with? What did you change?
4. What do you tell people when their input was not acted on, including when the decision was made overseas?

Landing the five parts:

| Part | How to land it |
|---|---|
| Why here | People asked twice, a loop left open for months, or a patient organisation that has stopped saying yes. |
| First move | Close one loop this month: go back to the last advisory board and tell its members what changed because of them. |
| 90-day action | That note sent; a feedback step added to the activity checklist; one shared insight record that all functions can read, with consent built in. |
| Owner | The person who ran the last advisory board for the note; the accountable leader for the shared record. |
| Measure | The note as sent, and the record with entries from more than one function. |

## Domain 7. Outcomes and Impact

Check question: "Name one protocol, endpoint, support program or evidence plan that changed because of patient input. How long did it take you to think of it?"

Probing questions:

1. What do you report on engagement now, and is any of it beyond what transparency reporting requires?
2. Which outcomes that patients told you matter are measured alongside clinical and commercial measures?
3. What do the patient organisations you work with receive from you about the engagement itself?
4. Has any patient organisation been asked what good partnership looks like, and whether you met it?

Landing the five parts:

| Part | How to land it |
|---|---|
| Why here | A leadership team or a global function asking what engagement delivers, with only activity counts to show. |
| First move | Ask three patient organisations one question: what would tell you we are a good partner? Start measuring it. |
| 90-day action | Three conversations held, two or three measures chosen from what was heard, first results shared with those organisations, and one changed decision written up in general terms. |
| Owner | The accountable leader asks the question in person. It is not delegated to an agency. |
| Measure | The measures, and confirmation from the three organisations that they received the results. |

Keep the written example general: the kind of decision that changed, never a product name and never anything connected to a submission.

## Affiliate patterns to listen for

- "Global decides that." Ask which part is decided locally, and work only on that part.
- "Compliance will not allow it." Ask what compliance was asked, by whom, and in what words. Often the question was never put.
- Four functions, four relationships with one patient organisation, no shared record.
- Engagement owned by a person with no budget and no seat at the leadership table.
- Activity that peaks near launch or reimbursement and falls away afterwards. Name the pattern; do not discuss the product.
- An advisory board treated as the whole of patient engagement.
