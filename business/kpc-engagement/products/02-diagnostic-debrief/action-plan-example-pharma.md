## Page 2. Priority one: one leader is accountable for patient engagement across the affiliate

*Illustrative example, not a real client.*

Domain: Purpose and Governance. Score: 2.0.

**Why it matters here**
The 2027-28 budget is set on 30 April. Without a named owner and a stated purpose, each function will bid for its own activity, and patient organisations will keep being approached four ways. Two of them have already said so.

**The first move**
Write one page for the leadership team: what patient engagement is for here, who is accountable, and the three decisions this year it must inform. The Patient Engagement Lead drafts it this week with the Medical Director and the Head of Market Access.

**The 90-day action**
The page adopted by the leadership team, with a named accountable leader and a single contact route for each patient organisation the affiliate works with.

| | |
|---|---|
| Owner | Medical Director |
| What it will take | Two half-days of drafting; 30 minutes on the 30 April agenda; no spend |
| Measure | The leadership team minute naming the owner, and the contact list |
| Check date | 30 April 2027 |

**Watch for**
The page being sent to the global team for comment before the local leadership team has seen it. The early sign is a draft with no date on it.

## Page 3. Priority two: people who advise you hear what happened to their advice

*Illustrative example, not a real client.*

Domain: Feedback and Learning. Score: 2.3.

**Why it matters here**
The members of your November advisory board have heard nothing since. You intend to ask some of them to join a standing panel. That request will land badly while the last loop is open.

**The first move**
Close one loop this month: go back to the last advisory board and tell its members what changed because of them. Include what you did not act on and why. Compliance agreed in the session to review the note within five working days.

**The 90-day action**
A feedback note is a standard, dated step in the activity checklist used by every function, and one shared record of patient insight exists that all four functions can read.

| | |
|---|---|
| Owner | Patient Engagement Lead |
| What it will take | Three hours for the note; one day to set up the shared record; compliance review time |
| Measure | The note as sent, and the checklist with the new step |
| Check date | 31 March 2027 for the note; 31 May 2027 for the checklist and record |

**Watch for**
The note becoming a thank-you letter. If it does not name one thing that changed and one that did not, it has not closed the loop.

## Page 4. Priority three: the leadership team has a costed proposal for a standing patient and carer panel

*Illustrative example, not a real client.*

Domain: Voice and Representation. Score: 2.5.

**Why it matters here**
You hear from patients only through one-off advisory boards drawn from two organisations. A standing panel is the one thing you want funded on 30 April, and at present there is nothing written to put in front of the meeting.

**The first move**
Set up one standing patient and carer panel with terms of reference, and put one real decision in front of it this quarter. In the session you adapted this: draft the terms of reference and the cost first, for approval on 30 April, and recruit only once funded.

**The 90-day action**
Terms of reference approved, six to eight members recruited including carers and at least two people from outside the organisations you already work with, and a first meeting date set with an open decision on its agenda.

| | |
|---|---|
| Owner | Patient Engagement Lead, with the Compliance Manager reviewing the terms |
| What it will take | Two days of drafting and recruitment. Member payments at fair market value under your existing policy, shown as a separate budget line: [Kris: this line stays a slot; the client supplies its own rates] |
| Measure | The approved terms, the membership by type (not by name), and the decision on the first agenda |
| Check date | 30 April 2027 for approval; 31 May 2027 for recruitment |

**Watch for**
Recruiting through the same two organisations because it is quick. The early sign is a member list with no carers on it.

## Page 5. What to stop

*Illustrative example, not a real client.*

A plan that only adds work does not get done. In the session you agreed to stop or pause the following.

| Stop or pause | Why | What it frees up | Who needs to be told, by whom, by when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pause new one-off advisory boards for 90 days, then review | They are the only mechanism in use, and the last one has an open loop | About four days of the Patient Engagement Lead's time, and the budget for one meeting | Function heads, by the Medical Director, by 12 March 2027 |
| Stop separate approaches to the same patient organisation by different functions | Two organisations have raised it | Goodwill, and duplicated effort | The patient organisations concerned, by the Patient Engagement Lead, by 31 March 2027 |

**How to tell people**
The patient organisations hear first, and they hear why. Suggested wording to adapt: "From now on you will have one contact here for everything. I am sorry it has taken this long. I will be in touch about anything already under way."

**Not now**
- Outcomes and Impact (2.0): revisit at the day 90 review, once there is an owner and a panel to ask what good partnership looks like.
- Engagement practice training for commercial and corporate affairs: revisit after 30 April.
- Earlier involvement in development: needs the global team; raise it once the one-page purpose is adopted.
