## Page 1. Where you stand

*Illustrative example, not a real client.*

**Overall result:** 2.1 out of 5. Level 2: Emerging.

There is genuine activity, and it is disconnected from decisions. Families trust you, they turn up, and they tell you a great deal. Very little of it is written down, the people who give their time are thanked but not recognised under any policy, and they rarely hear what came of it. A low score is a starting line, not a verdict. With three people and a volunteer board, the aim for 90 days is three small things finished, not seven things started.

**Your two strongest domains, and what they make possible**
- Purpose and Governance: the board already agrees why families are involved, so a policy will not need a long argument.

- Feedback and Learning: families who answered last year's survey have heard nothing, which makes the next request harder.

## Page 2. Priority one: families who gave their views hear what happened to them

*Illustrative example, not a real client.*

Domain: Feedback and Learning. Score: 1.8.

**Why it matters here**
Last year's survey went to every family on your list and the results were never shared. The funder report asks what changed because of community input. You cannot answer that for the funder before you have answered it for the families.

**The first move**
Close one loop this month: go back to the last group consulted and tell them what happened to their input. In the session you agreed on one page in the April newsletter: three things families said, what you did about each, and one thing you could not do and why.

**The 90-day action**
A single shared document, "What we heard and what we did", with an entry for every piece of engagement since January 2026, kept by the Coordinator and tabled at each board meeting.

| | |
|---|---|
| Owner | Community Programs Coordinator |
| What it will take | Four hours for the newsletter page; one hour a month for the document; no spend |
| Measure | The newsletter page as published, and the document with dated entries |
| Check date | 15 April 2027 |

**Watch for**
Waiting until the survey has been "properly analysed". Three honest points this month are worth more than a full analysis in August.

## Page 3. Priority two: community members are recognised for their time under a written policy

*Illustrative example, not a real client.*

Domain: Safety, Ethics and Recognition. Score: 1.5.

**Why it matters here**
Parents speak at events, review materials and share their stories, unpaid, and their stories have been reused in fundraising without asking again. The funder report asks about recognition, and the 2027-28 budget goes to the board on 20 May.

**The first move**
Adopt and publish a recognition and payment policy for community members, even a modest one. You agreed to start with one page: what is paid or recognised, what costs are covered, how consent for stories works, and who to contact if taking part is distressing.

**The 90-day action**
The policy adopted by the board on 20 May 2027, published on your website, and a line for community member payments in the 2027-28 budget.

| | |
|---|---|
| Owner | Chief Executive, with the Board Chair taking it to the board |
| What it will take | One day of drafting; 20 minutes of board time. Community member payments at a published rate schedule, shown as a separate budget line: [Kris: name the published rate schedule you cite] |
| Measure | The policy on the website, and the budget line in the adopted budget |
| Check date | 20 May 2027 |

**Watch for**
The policy stalling because the budget line looks unaffordable. A modest policy that is kept is better than a generous one that is not adopted. The early sign is the item slipping off the May agenda.

## Page 4. Priority three: a small advisory group has one real decision in front of it

*Illustrative example, not a real client.*

Domain: Voice and Representation. Score: 2.0.

**Why it matters here**
The same four parents are asked for everything, and they are tired. Adults living with the condition, fathers and families outside the capital cities are not heard at all. The funder will ask who was involved, and "the people we already knew" is a weak answer.

**The first move**
Stand up one small advisory group with clear terms of reference and one real decision on its agenda. You agreed to keep it to six people, meeting online three times a year, with terms of one page.

**The 90-day action**
Terms of reference written, six members invited including at least two people you have not heard from before, and a first meeting held on the 2027-28 program plan while it is still in draft.

| | |
|---|---|
| Owner | Community Programs Coordinator, with a board member attending |
| What it will take | One day to write the terms and invite people; 90 minutes for the meeting. Members recognised under the new policy, as a separate line |
| Measure | The terms, the membership by type (not by name), and one change to the draft program plan that came from the meeting |
| Check date | 7 June 2027 |

**Watch for**
Inviting the same four parents because they always say yes. Ask each of them to suggest one person instead.

## Page 5. What to stop

*Illustrative example, not a real client.*

A plan that only adds work does not get done. In the session you agreed to stop or pause the following.

| Stop or pause | Why | What it frees up | Who needs to be told, by whom, by when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stop the annual all-family survey in its current form | Nobody has time to analyse it, and it has not changed a decision | About three days of the Coordinator's time a year | Families, in the April newsletter page, by the Coordinator |
| Pause requests for family stories for fundraising until the policy is adopted | No consent process, no recognition | Goodwill, and the time of the same four parents | The fundraising volunteer, by the Chief Executive, by 19 March 2027 |

**How to tell people**
Families hear first, in the same newsletter page that reports back on the survey. Suggested wording to adapt: "You told us a lot last year and it took us too long to tell you what we did with it. We are replacing the big survey with a small group that meets three times a year, and we will always tell you what came of it."

**Not now**
- An induction for community members: revisit once the advisory group has met, and ask its members what they wish they had been told.
- Measuring an outcome families care about: put the question to the advisory group at its second meeting.
- A named board member for engagement: raise at the board's planning day later in the year.

> **What makes this example good (internal):**
> - Every action fits a three-person team: hours, not weeks, and no new spend except payments to community members.
> - Priorities are ordered by the date of the decision each affects, not by score.
> - Each first move is the wording in `../CEOF-REFERENCE.md`, followed by how the client adapted it.
> - Seven actions, not nine. One happens this week, and it is a stop.
> - Stopping the survey is itself reported back to families.
> - Page 6 says honestly that no sprint is needed. Target conversion is 1 in 3.
> - The day 30 check-in (8 April) goes without the sprint paragraph, because page 6 named no decision.
