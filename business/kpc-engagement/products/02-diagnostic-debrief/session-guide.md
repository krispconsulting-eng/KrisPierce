# Diagnostic Debrief: session guide

Internal. Kris's script for the 90 minutes. Have open: the client's diagnostic report, the questionnaire answers, and `action-plan-template.md` with the cover block and results table already filled in. Take notes straight into the template.

The session produces three things and nothing else: three priorities, what to stop, and owners with dates. Say so at the start.

## 1. Agenda, minute by minute

| Minutes | Segment | What Kris does | What goes into the plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Open | Welcome. Confirm who is in the room and who can agree to owners and dates. State the three outputs. Say the plan arrives within five working days. | Cover block: attendees |
| 3 to 5 | The decision | Read back the decision or deadline from questionnaire question 1. "Is that still the one?" Everything after this is tested against it. | Cover block: the decision |
| 5 to 10 | The overall picture | Give the overall score and level name in one sentence, then the pattern: strongest two, weakest two. "A low score is a starting line, not a verdict." Ask: "Does that match what you see from the inside?" | Page 1: reading of the results |
| 10 to 15 | What surprised them | Take questionnaire questions 4 and 5. Ask each person for one sentence. Note any score the room agrees is too high or too low. | Page 1: what the session changed |
| 15 to 25 | The seven domains, quickly | About 80 seconds each. For each: read the score, ask the one check question from section 3, record "agrees" or the correction. Do not solve anything yet. | Page 1: results table, "what I heard" column |
| 25 to 30 | Lock the three priorities | Use the rule in section 2. Say the three aloud and get a yes from the decision-maker. | Pages 2 to 4: titles |
| 30 to 45 | Priority one | Use that domain's question set (section 3). Land the five parts: why here, first move, 90-day action, owner, measure. | Page 2 |
| 45 to 60 | Priority two | Same. | Page 3 |
| 60 to 72 | Priority three | Same, tighter. If time is short, land the first move, the owner and the date, and finish the rest in writing. | Page 4 |
| 72 to 80 | What to stop | Use section 4. Get at least one, at most three. | Page 5 |
| 80 to 87 | The 90 days and outside help | Read the nine or fewer actions back with owners and dates. Ask: "Which of these three can your team carry alone? Is there one that hinges on a decision nobody has made yet?" | Page 6 |
| 87 to 90 | Close | Name the action that happens this week and who does it. Confirm the plan date and the 30-day check-in. Thank them and stop on time. | Page 6: first action |

Timekeeping rules:

- Say the time aloud at 30, 60 and 80 minutes.
- If a priority runs over by five minutes, park the open point in the notes with "to settle in writing" and move on.
- Never extend the session. An overrun is settled in the plan.

## 2. How to land three priorities

Default: the three lowest-scoring domains, as the CEOF Diagnostic reports them.

Swap one out only if one of these is true, and say which rule applied:

1. **The deadline rule.** A different domain decides whether the client meets the decision or deadline in the cover block. That domain comes in.
2. **The foundation rule.** A low domain cannot move until another one does. Example: Engagement Practice will not hold if nobody is accountable under Purpose and Governance. The foundation comes in.
3. **The wrong-score rule.** The room agrees a low score was a misreading, and can say what evidence shows it. That domain goes out and the next lowest comes in.

Never more than three. If the client asks for a fourth, ask which of the three it replaces.

Order them by the date of the decision each one affects, earliest first. Not by score.

For each priority, land five parts before moving on. Ask them in this order:

| Part | The question Kris asks | Good enough when |
|---|---|---|
| Why it matters here | "What does this put at risk for [the decision]?" | It names a decision, obligation or relationship specific to this organisation |
| The first move | "The diagnostic suggests [first move from the table below]. Would that work here, as is, or with what change?" | One action that can start within two weeks |
| The 90-day action | "By day 90, what exists that does not exist today?" | A thing someone could be shown: a document, a group, a decision, a payment made |
| Owner | "Whose name goes next to it?" | One named person in or known to the room, who says yes |
| Measure | "How would someone outside your organisation know it was done?" | Checkable without taking anyone's word for it |

If no one will own a priority, it is not a priority. Say that, and choose again.

## 3. Questions by domain

Use the check question for every domain in minutes 15 to 25. Use the full set only for the three priorities. The first move is taken word for word from the CEOF workflow; adapt it with the client, do not replace it.

### Domain 1. Purpose and Governance

Check question: "If I asked your board chair why this organisation involves its community, what would I hear?"

When it is a priority:

1. Who is accountable for community involvement today, by name? What happens if that person leaves?
2. Where is the reason for involving your community written down, and when was it last read by the board or executive?
3. When did the board or leadership team last discuss engagement as an agenda item, not as an update?
4. Is there a budget line for it? Who approves spending against it?
5. Which decision in the next 12 months would the board want community input on, if it were offered?

Listen for: engagement that depends on one committed person; no budget line; a purpose statement nobody can quote.

First move: Take a one-page proposal to the next board meeting: why the organisation involves its community, who is accountable, and what it needs.

### Domain 2. Voice and Representation

Check question: "Who do you hear from now, and who do you never hear from?"

When it is a priority:

1. Which community members have a standing way to be heard, and how were they chosen?
2. Who is missing: by condition, age, location, language, carers as distinct from patients, people who are not already connected to you?
3. Do the people you hear from speak for themselves, or are they expected to speak for everyone?
4. What real decision has a community group had in front of it in the last 12 months?
5. Are there terms of reference: purpose, term, time expected, payment, how advice is used?

Listen for: the same two or three people asked every time; a group with meetings but no decisions; no terms of reference.

First move: Stand up one small advisory group with clear terms of reference and one real decision on its agenda.

### Domain 3. Capability and Culture

Check question: "When a community member joins a meeting here for the first time, what happens before they walk in?"

When it is a priority:

1. What do community members receive before their first involvement: who is who, what is being decided, what is expected of them?
2. What have staff been told or taught about working with community members? Who taught it?
3. Which staff do this well already, and what do they do differently?
4. Where has it gone badly, and what was missing?
5. Does anyone's role description or performance plan mention it?

Listen for: goodwill without preparation; community members briefed in the lift; staff who see involvement as someone else's job.

First move: Build a simple induction for community members and a one-session briefing for staff.

### Domain 4. Engagement Practice

Check question: "Think of your last piece of engagement. What decision was it for?"

When it is a priority:

1. At what point in a project does someone ask whether the community should be involved? Who asks?
2. For your last three engagement activities: what was the decision, and was it still open when you asked people?
3. How do you choose a method: a survey, a group, interviews, a co-design session?
4. What do you do with what you hear? Who reads it, and by when?
5. Which project starting in the next 90 days could be the test case?

Listen for: activity chosen before the decision is named; consultation after the decision is made; every question answered with a survey.

First move: Add one question to project planning: what decision is this, and what involvement does it warrant?

### Domain 5. Safety, Ethics and Recognition

Check question: "Are community members paid for their time, and is the rate written down anywhere?"

When it is a priority:

1. What do you pay, to whom, and under which published rate schedule? How long does payment take to arrive?
2. Do you cover costs: travel, care, access needs, data?
3. How do people consent to taking part, and how do they withdraw? What happens to what they told you?
4. When someone becomes distressed telling their story, what is the plan? Who follows up afterwards?
5. Who decides whether an activity needs ethics review, and how?

Listen for: gift cards in place of payment; payment that depends on who asks; no plan for distress; stories reused without asking again.

First move: Adopt and publish a recognition and payment policy for community members, even a modest one.

Note: any action here that involves consumers is costed with payments at published rates as a separate line.

### Domain 6. Feedback and Learning

Check question: "The last group you consulted: do they know what happened to what they told you?"

When it is a priority:

1. After an engagement activity, who goes back to participants, with what, and by when?
2. Can you name one thing that changed because of community input, and did the people who gave it hear about it?
3. When input was not acted on, were people told why?
4. Do you review your own engagement after each activity? What have you changed as a result?
5. Which open loop is the oldest?

Listen for: thank-you emails standing in for feedback; changes made but never reported back; no record of what was heard.

First move: Close one loop this month: go back to the last group consulted and tell them what happened to their input.

### Domain 7. Outcomes and Impact

Check question: "What do you report about engagement: what you did, or what changed?"

When it is a priority:

1. What do you count now: sessions, attendees, survey responses?
2. What has changed in a decision, a program or a document because of community involvement? How do you know?
3. Has anyone asked the community what result they care about?
4. Who asks you for evidence of engagement: funders, reviewers, partners, the board? What do they accept?
5. What is one outcome you could start measuring this quarter with data you already hold?

Listen for: counts of activity presented as impact; no community-defined outcome; evidence assembled the week a report is due.

First move: Ask the community one question: what outcome would tell you we are doing our job? Start measuring it.

## 4. How to land what to stop

Start from questionnaire question 6, starred items first.

Ask, in this order:

1. "Which of these activities has not changed a decision in the last 12 months?"
2. "Which one costs your community the most time for the least return to them?"
3. "If you stopped it tomorrow, who would notice, and what would they lose?"
4. "What does stopping it free up: hours, budget, goodwill?"
5. "Who needs to be told, and who tells them?"

Rules:

- At least one, at most three. A plan that only adds work will not be carried out.
- "Pause for 90 days and review" is an acceptable answer if "stop" is too hard to say in the room.
- Stopping an activity that involves community members is itself a loop to close. The plan records who tells them and what they are told.
- Common candidates: a survey nobody analyses; a group that meets without a decision on its agenda; asking the same few people for everything; unpaid requests for stories; reporting activity counts nobody reads.

If the client cannot name anything, offer this: "Then the thing to stop is starting new engagement activity until priority one is done." Record it if they agree.

## 5. Lines to have ready

- When the scores are challenged: "The score is where the conversation starts. Tell me what I would see if I looked, and I will record your reading."
- When the conversation widens: "That matters, and it is outside the three. I will note it on page 6 so it is not lost."
- When no owner steps forward: "If nobody owns it, it does not go in the plan. Should it come out?"
- When asked for more than the debrief covers: "That is more than a debrief can carry. It is what a sprint is for, and I will say so in the plan if I think it is worth it."
- When a product or application before a committee comes up: "I do not work on anything tied to an application before a committee I sit on or advise, so I will leave that outside this session. Everything about your general engagement capability is in scope."

## 6. Straight after the session (10 minutes, inside the step 5 writing time)

- Fill any gaps in the template while the wording is fresh.
- Mark anything recorded as "to settle in writing".
- Decide the page 6 answer now: is there one decision worth a sprint, yes or no, and which sprint type.
- Diarise the plan due date and the 30-day check-in.
