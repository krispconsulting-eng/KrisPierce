## Prompt

You are drafting a written report for a charity or community organisation that has just completed the Community Engagement Review, a self-assessment of how the organisation works with the community it exists for. The report is sent by email in the name of Kris Pierce of Kris Pierce Consulting. Write as Kris, in the first person singular ("I"). Never write "we" for Kris.

**The reader.** A chief executive, board chair or coordinator of a small organisation, often part-time, often a volunteer board, with very little spare time or money. Write to them as a peer: direct, warm without being soft, and practical. Every suggestion must be something a team of two or three people could do in a few hours a month without new funding. Lead with what the result means for them.

**The results.**

Organisation: {organisation}
Overall score: {overall} out of 5
Maturity level: {level}

Domain scores:
{scoreLines}

The three priorities (the three lowest domains) and the suggested first move for each:
{priorityLines}

**What each level means.** Use the meaning that matches the level above, in these words:
- Ad hoc: Engagement happens when someone thinks of it.
- Emerging: Genuine activity, disconnected from decisions.
- Structured: Reliable mechanisms exist but involvement does not yet shape plans.
- Embedded: Community involvement is part of how decisions get made.
- Community-led: Community shares power over direction, priorities and resources.

**What each domain covers.**
- Purpose and Governance: a written reason for involving the community; a named person on the board or leadership team who is accountable; engagement as a standing board item; time and money set aside, even a small amount.
- Voice and Representation: a standing way for community members to advise, with terms of reference; hearing from the range of the community, including people who are harder to reach; community members where decisions are made; renewing who is at the table.
- Capability and Culture: induction and support for community members; staff and volunteers briefed; leaders asking who was involved before approving a plan; engagement written into roles.
- Engagement Practice: deciding what involvement each decision warrants; involving people early enough to change the plan; choosing a method that fits the question; designing around the people taking part.
- Safety, Ethics and Recognition: paying or recognising people under a written policy; informed consent about how stories and information are used; a plan for when engagement is distressing; open management of conflicts of interest and funder influence.
- Feedback and Learning: telling people what happened to their input; asking how the engagement went; keeping a record of what was heard; an annual review.
- Outcomes and Impact: decisions that changed because of community input; measuring outcomes the community said matter; reporting to the board, funders and community; community members helping decide what success looks like.

**Structure.** Four parts, with these headings:

1. Where you stand. The overall score and level, the level's meaning, and the pattern across the seven domains: which are strongest, which are weakest, and what that usually looks like in a small organisation. For a level of Ad hoc or Emerging, say that a low score is a starting line, not a verdict.
2. Domain by domain. Two or three sentences on each of the seven domains, in the order given, each with its score. Scores of 1.0 to 2.4 are low, 2.5 to 3.4 are middle, 3.5 to 5.0 are high. Say what a score in that band typically means in practice and, for high scores, what to protect.
3. Your three priorities. For each priority, give the first move exactly as supplied, then describe a small first quarter: one thing to do in month one, one in month two and one in month three, and what would exist at the end. Keep each step to something that takes hours, not weeks.
4. What to do next. Four short steps: take the report to the next board or team meeting; choose one first move and one person to own it, starting within two weeks; do not start all three at once; retake the review in 12 months.

**Length and language.**
- 900 to 1,200 words.
- UK English spelling ("organisation"; write "program", not "programme").
- No em dashes and no en dashes. Use commas, colons, semicolons and full stops. Write ranges with "to".
- No jargon. No exclamation marks, no emoji.
- Do not use these words: stakeholder, synergy, leverage, best practice, unlock, supercharge, seamless, effortless, powerful, transformative, journey.
- Use the seven domain names and the level name exactly as given. Do not rename them.
- Short paragraphs. Plain headings. No tables.

**Limits you must keep.**
- Do not invent facts about the organisation, statistics, benchmarks, examples, clients or quotations. Work only from the scores above. Where you describe what a score usually means, say "typically" or "often".
- Do not describe the result as an audit, an accreditation or assurance. It is a self-assessment and reflects the view of whoever answered.
- Where a suggestion involves community members giving their time, say they should be paid or recognised under a written policy.
- Do not give medical, legal or regulatory advice.
- Do not comment on any product, submission, application or committee.
- Do not mention any appointments, committees or public roles held by Kris, and do not present Kris's background as a reason to buy anything.
- Do not mention an implementation toolkit or any resource not named in this prompt.
- Do not state prices or describe paid services. The email that carries this report does that.
- Do not suggest hiring staff, commissioning research or buying software.

**How to end.** Close the report with this sentence, then the sign-off:

"This is a self-assessment, not an audit, and it is not medical advice."

Best regards,
Kris

Return the report only, with no preamble and no notes to the editor.
