# The Engagement Review: two versions

Draft for Kris's approval. Added 15 November 2026. Site in scope: krispierce.com.au only.

The diagnostic now opens with a choice of two reviews. Both sit on the same Community Engagement Operating Framework: the same seven domains, the same five maturity levels, the same scoring. The statements, the meaning of each level and the first moves are written for each setting. Each has its own link, so it can be sent directly.

| Version | Public name | Link | For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pharmaceutical | Patient Engagement Review | `/diagnostic#pharma` | Pharmaceutical, biotech and medtech companies: how the company works with patients, carers and patient organisations |
| Charity | Community Engagement Review | `/diagnostic#charities` | Charities and community organisations: how the organisation works with the community it exists for |

"Engagement Review" is the public name. "CEOF" stays as the name of the framework underneath, which matters less to a buyer than what the review tells them.

## Patient Engagement Review (pharmaceutical)

The one that matters most commercially. It is written so that a patient engagement lead, a medical director or a general manager can answer it in ten minutes and recognise their own company in the result.

### What it tests that the charity version does not

- Whether engagement is owned across the affiliate or split between medical, access, commercial and corporate affairs.
- Whether patients are involved from early development or only near launch and reimbursement.
- Whether local input reaches global teams in time to change anything.
- Whether payment is at fair market value under one policy.
- Whether funded patient organisations keep their independence.
- Whether the company reports back beyond what transparency rules require.

### The 28 statements

**1. Purpose and Governance**
1. We have a written patient engagement strategy that says what engagement is for and which decisions it must inform.
2. One senior leader is accountable for patient engagement across the affiliate, not only within one function.
3. The leadership team reviews patient engagement on a set schedule, and it has its own budget.
4. Medical, market access, commercial and corporate affairs have agreed who does what, so a patient organisation is not approached four ways.

**2. Voice and Representation**
1. We have a standing way to hear from patients and carers, such as a panel or council, not only one-off advisory boards.
2. We hear from the range of people affected, including carers and people outside the best-resourced patient organisations.
3. Patient insight is in the room when trial design, evidence plans and support programs are decided.
4. Local patient input reaches global teams early enough to change something, and we can show when it did.

**3. Capability and Culture**
1. Staff who work with patients and patient organisations are trained in engagement practice, not only in compliance.
2. We brief and support patients and carers properly for the roles we ask them to take.
3. Leaders ask what patients said before they approve a plan.
4. Engagement is planned and resourced in medical, access and brand plans from the start.

**4. Engagement Practice**
1. We involve patients from early development and trial design, not only near launch or reimbursement.
2. For each activity we decide what decision it serves and what level of involvement that decision warrants.
3. We choose the method to fit the question, and use independent facilitation where our presence would shape the answers.
4. Participation is designed around people: timing, format, plain language and caring responsibilities.

**5. Safety, Ethics and Recognition**
1. We pay patients and carers fair market value under one written policy, applied the same way across functions, and reimburse costs promptly.
2. All engagement is non-promotional and within the Medicines Australia Code of Conduct, and staff know where the lines are.
3. Patient organisations we fund keep their independence: support is transparent, never tied to a position, and unrestricted where possible.
4. Consent, privacy and adverse event reporting are built into every activity, and participants know how their input will be used.

**6. Feedback and Learning**
1. We tell patients and patient organisations what we did with their input, including what we did not act on and why.
2. We ask patient organisations what we are like to work with, and change in response.
3. Insight is recorded and shared across functions, so the same people are not asked the same questions twice.
4. We review our engagement approach at least once a year.

**7. Outcomes and Impact**
1. We can point to decisions (a protocol, an endpoint, a support program, an evidence plan) that changed because of patient input.
2. We measure outcomes that patients told us matter, alongside clinical and commercial measures.
3. We report on our engagement to the patient organisations involved, beyond the transparency reporting the Code requires.
4. Patient organisations help define what good partnership looks like and judge whether we met it.

### First moves (new; the workflow's first moves were written for charities)

| Domain | First move |
|---|---|
| Purpose and Governance | Write one page for the leadership team: what patient engagement is for here, who is accountable, and the three decisions this year it must inform. |
| Voice and Representation | Set up one standing patient and carer panel with terms of reference, and put one real decision in front of it this quarter. |
| Capability and Culture | Run one session for every team that meets patients: engagement practice first, compliance second. |
| Engagement Practice | Add one line to every plan template: what decision is this, and when did patients inform it? |
| Safety, Ethics and Recognition | Write down your payment and independence principles for patients and patient organisations, publish them, and apply them the same way in every function. |
| Feedback and Learning | Close one loop this month: go back to the last advisory board and tell its members what changed because of them. |
| Outcomes and Impact | Ask three patient organisations one question: what would tell you we are a good partner? Start measuring it. |

### What each level means for a company

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ad hoc | Patient engagement happens when a project needs it. |
| Emerging | There is real activity, but it sits in pockets and rarely reaches decisions. |
| Structured | Reliable mechanisms exist, but patient input does not yet shape plans. |
| Embedded | Patient input is part of how development, evidence and support decisions get made. |
| Community-led | Patients and patient organisations share in setting priorities and judging results. |

### Boundaries built into the page

The pharmaceutical version shows two lines the charity version does not, before the first question and again beside the debrief offer:

> This review looks at how your company engages, as a capability. It does not assess any product, and I do not work on product-specific submissions or advise on how a committee is likely to respond.

> My government advisory appointments are held independently of this practice. Clients receive no access to those bodies or their deliberations.

Run the conflict check in `../../assets/proposal-template.md` before any debrief with a company.

### Landing copy

**Headline:** How well does your company work with patients?

**Body:** Most companies can list their patient activities. Fewer can say which decisions those activities changed. The Patient Engagement Review takes ten minutes and shows where your engagement stands across seven domains, from who is accountable for it to whether patient organisations would call you a good partner. You see the result straight away, with three priorities and a first move for each.

**Who should take it:** the person accountable for patient engagement or advocacy, with medical affairs or market access alongside if you can. Two people answering separately and comparing results is more useful than one.

**What it is not:** an audit, a compliance check, or advice on any product or submission.

### Where it sits in the sales path

Review (free) → Debrief ($1,500) → Advisory Sprint ($12,000) on the top priority → Ongoing Strategic Advisor. For companies the most likely sprint topics are an engagement strategy for the affiliate, a payment and independence policy, and a standing patient panel. All are capability work and inside the guardrails.

## Community Engagement Review (charity)

The original 28 statements, lightly reworded for organisations run by small teams and volunteers. Three changes:

- "A named person on the board or leadership team is accountable" (was "at leadership level").
- "Time and money are set aside for engagement, even a small amount" (was "has its own budget line", which few small charities could say yes to).
- "Staff and volunteers have been briefed" (was "staff have been trained or briefed").

The full set is in `PRODUCT.md`, with those three lines as now shown on the page. The first moves are Kris's originals from the workflow, unchanged.

**Headline:** How well do you work with your community?

**Body:** Funders and partners now ask charities to show how the people they serve shape what they do. The Community Engagement Review takes ten minutes and shows where you stand across seven domains, with three priorities and a first move for each. A low result is a starting line, not a verdict.

Charity rate applies to what follows: 25% off the sprint and training for organisations with revenue under $1 million.

- Tested in a browser on 15 November 2026: both versions run end to end, validation works, scores and priorities compute correctly, and the phone layout holds. Not tested: sending to the webhook, which is switched off.
