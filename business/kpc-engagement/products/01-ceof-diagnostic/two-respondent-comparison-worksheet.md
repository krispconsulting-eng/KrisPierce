> **What this is:** a one-page worksheet for two people in the same organisation who each took the review separately. It can be sent as it stands. Works for both the Patient Engagement Review and the Community Engagement Review.

## 2. How to read the gaps

| Gap | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Under 0.5 | You see it the same way. | Accept the result. If both scores are low, it is a shared priority. |
| 0.5 to 0.9 | A difference of emphasis. | Note it and move on, unless the domain is in either person's lowest three. |
| 1.0 to 1.9 | One of you knows about something the other does not, or you are judging against different standards. | Talk about it today. Ask the higher scorer for one example, and the lower scorer what they would need to see. |
| 2.0 or more | You are describing two different organisations. Often one function, team or program does this well and another does not. | This is your first conversation, ahead of any low score. Work out whether practice differs across the organisation or only the view of it. |

Three things to keep in mind:

- A gap of one point or more matters more than a low score you both agree on. An agreed low score is a known problem. A gap is an unknown one.
- The higher score is not the optimistic one and the lower score is not the honest one. The person closer to the work often scores lower because they see the exceptions; the person further away may know about a policy the other has never been shown.
- If one of you scored a domain at 4 or above and the other at 2.5 or below, check whether the thing exists on paper but not in practice.

## 3. Five questions to discuss

1. **Where is the widest gap, and what does each of you know that the other does not?** Give one real example each.
2. **Which low score do you both agree on?** If you could move only that one this quarter, what would you do first, and who would own it?
3. **Is the difference about practice or about visibility?** Does one part of the organisation do this well, or does good work happen that the other person never hears about?
4. **Who would a third person agree with?** Name someone whose view you both trust, such as a community member, a patient organisation you work with or a colleague in another team. What would they say?
5. **What decision in the next six months does this affect?** If you cannot name one, which result would an outsider notice first?
