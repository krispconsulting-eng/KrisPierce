# Ongoing Strategic Advisor: product definition

> **What this is:** the internal definition of the retainer, so it is sold and delivered the same way every time. Client-facing wording is marked. Prices are AUD, excluding GST, as set in `../../BRAIN.md` section 4.
> **Status:** draft for Kris to confirm. Terms marked [Kris] or [lawyer] need a decision or a legal check before first use.

## 1. Promise (client-facing)

For six months you have an independent adviser on consumer engagement who knows your organisation, your people and the three decisions that matter this half-year. You get a working session every month, written review of the documents that carry those decisions, and a quick answer when something comes up between sessions. Every month you get one page recording what was decided and what I advised.

I advise. Your team does the work and makes the decisions. If my advice is not changing decisions by the first quarterly review, I will say so and recommend you stop.

## 2. Who it is for

| Segment | Typical situation |
|---|---|
| Organisations that have finished a Consumer Engagement Design project or a Strategic Advisory Sprint | Running a new framework or plan for the first time and wanting a steady hand while it beds in |
| Biopharma, biotech and healthtech in rare disease | A patient engagement or advocacy lead building a function without senior peers to test thinking with, inside the Medicines Australia Code of Conduct |
| Research institutes and trial networks | A funded program with consumer involvement commitments to deliver across several projects |
| Patient organisations | A chief executive or board chair preparing for research and industry partnerships |

The usual route in is from a sprint or a design project. A retainer sold cold, with no prior piece of work, starts with a longer onboarding session (see `onboarding-kit.md`).

## 4. What is excluded

- Product-specific work on any application that is, or is likely to be, before a committee I sit on or advise. No drafting, reviewing or coaching on a sponsor's submission, including any consumer input section of one. No advice on how a committee is likely to respond.
- Access to, or information about, any public body or its deliberations.
- Producing deliverables. I review and advise on your documents; I do not write them. A decision paper is a Strategic Advisory Sprint. A framework is a Consumer Engagement Design project.
- Facilitating sessions with consumers, recruiting consumers, or holding participant data. Where your work involves consumers, they are paid by you at published rates as a separate line in your own budget, never out of my fee.
- Review of promotional material, or of anything naming a product for a promotional purpose.
- Acting as your spokesperson, representing you to government, funders or other organisations, or making introductions to decision-makers.
- Staff management, recruitment decisions or performance matters.
- Medical, legal or regulatory advice.
- Urgent, same-day or out-of-hours response.

## 5. Unused time

Recommended rule, and the one written into the agreement:

- Unused time carries over for one month only, up to 0.5 day on the 1.5-day tier and 1 day on the 3-day tier.
- Carried-over time is used first in the following month. Anything still unused at the end of that month lapses.
- Time is not refunded and is not carried past the end of the term.
- If less than half the time is used for two months in a row, I raise it at the next session and recommend resizing or stopping. A retainer that is not being used is not doing its job.
- Time lost because I was unavailable (illness, leave agreed in advance) is carried over in full until used. [Kris: confirm.]

Why one month: it lets a quiet month feed a busy one without building a bank of days that lands on the calendar all at once and breaks the capacity rule.

## 6. Scope creep

The retainer covers advice on the decisions in the advisory brief. Three tests, applied in this order whenever a request arrives:

1. **Is it advice, or is it a deliverable?** If you need me to produce something, it is a project. I will say so and quote a Strategic Advisory Sprint ($12,000), a Consumer Engagement Design project ($18,000 to $45,000) or a training day ($9,500).
2. **Is it one of the three decisions?** If not, I answer briefly once, and raise at the next session whether the advisory brief should change. The brief can be changed at any quarterly review, or once mid-term on the 3-day tier.
3. **Does it fit this month's time?** If the month's time is used, the request waits until next month, or you can buy extra time: up to 1 extra day in any month at $2,400, agreed in writing before the work is done. More than that for two months running means the tier is wrong, and I will recommend moving up or scoping a project.

What I say when it happens: "That is outside what the retainer covers. I can do one of three things: give you a short view now, hold it for the next session, or scope it as a separate piece of work. Which would be most useful?"

Excess is never billed after the fact. No surprise invoices.

## 8. Capacity rule

No more than two retainers at once, and no more than one project alongside them. When both places are taken, a prospect is offered a start date, not a third place. If demand continues, the price goes up before days are added.

Before accepting a second retainer, run the between-clients check in `conflict-and-boundaries.md`.

## 9. Fit test: who should not buy this

Say no, or offer something else, when any of these is true:

| Sign | Offer instead |
|---|---|
| There is one decision with a deadline | Strategic Advisory Sprint |
| They need something built: a framework, a plan, a set of sessions | Consumer Engagement Design |
| They need staff who can run co-design | In-house co-design training |
| No one senior enough to act on advice will attend the monthly session | Decline; revisit when there is a decision owner |
| They want an extra pair of hands, not an adviser | Decline; suggest hiring or contracting |
| They want the retainer for who I know or what bodies I sit on | Decline, and say why |
| They have a product that is, or is likely to be, before a committee I sit on or advise, and the retainer could not be kept clearly separate from it | Decline |
| They cannot name three decisions for the next six months | Diagnostic Debrief first |
| They want urgent, same-day response | Decline; this is not that service |
| They are a direct competitor of a current retainer client in the same condition area | Decline or defer [Kris: confirm this rule] |

Questions to ask on the fit call:

1. What are the three decisions in the next six months where an outside view would change what you do?
2. Who will be in the monthly session, and can they act on what is discussed?
3. What have you tried already?
4. What would make you end this after three months?
5. Is there any product, application or negotiation I should know about before I run a conflict check?

## 10. Company clients

For pharmaceutical, biotech and medical technology companies the retainer sits inside the Medicines Australia Code of Conduct, and the proposal includes section 10 of the proposal template. In practice:

- No product promotion. I do not review promotional material or make product claims.
- Advice is about general engagement capability: structures, policies, methods, payment, safety, feedback. It is not tied to a live or likely application.
- Consumer contact stays at arm's length. I do not introduce the company to individual consumers, and the company sends me no individual consumer data. Documents for review are de-identified before they reach me.
- The fee is fair market value for the work, under a written agreement, and may be declared by me where my other roles require it.
- Kris Pierce Consulting is a business, not a patient organisation, and payments to it are not reported as support for one.

## 11. How it ends well

A retainer should end on purpose, not drift.

- **Month 5:** the renewal conversation (see `emails.md` and `quarterly-review-template.md`). Three options are on the table every time: renew, resize, stop.
- **If stopping:** a final session of the usual length that covers what changed, what is unfinished and who owns it, and what to watch for in the next six months. A closing note of up to two pages. Return or deletion of your documents within 30 days.
- **After it ends:** no ongoing obligation either way. Confidentiality continues. I suggest a CEOF Diagnostic re-assessment at 12 months from your baseline.
- **Good reasons to stop:** the three decisions are made; your team no longer needs the outside view; the advice is not changing decisions. I will recommend stopping when I see any of them.
- With written consent, a named case for the service page. No client is named without it.
