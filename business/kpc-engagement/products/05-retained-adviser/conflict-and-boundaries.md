# Ongoing Strategic Advisor: conflict and boundaries

> **What this is:** the standing conflict check for every retainer, what I stop and say if a conflict arises, how confidentiality works between clients, and the independence line. It applies the rules in `../../../01-GUARDRAILS.md` to a relationship that runs for months, where circumstances change.
> **Status:** drafted for Kris to confirm and for a lawyer to review. Not legal advice.

## 1. The independence line

This sentence appears, word for word, in every proposal, every advisory brief and the welcome email:

> My government advisory appointments are held independently of this practice. Clients receive no access to those bodies or their deliberations.

What it means in practice:

- I do not describe, hint at or draw on anything I know from those appointments that is not public.
- I do not offer or imply introductions, influence or insight into how any body or its members think.
- I do not use the appointments as a reason to engage me. If a prospect raises them as the reason, I correct it, and if that was the reason, I decline.
- I do not do product-specific work on any application that is, or is likely to be, before a committee I sit on or advise: no drafting, reviewing or coaching on a sponsor's submission, and no advice on how a committee is likely to respond.
- I can explain how the Australian system works from public sources, and help an organisation build its general engagement capability.
- Where my roles require it, I declare the engagement and the fee. Clients agree to that on acceptance.

## 2. The standing conflict check

Run at onboarding (before the proposal is sent), at each quarterly review, and whenever something changes. Each run is dated and recorded: at onboarding in section 9 of the proposal, each quarter in section 6 of the quarterly review, and in the pipeline tracker.

### Part A: the client and public processes

| # | Question | If yes |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Does the client have a product, technology or service that is, or is likely within 12 months to be, before MSAC, PBAC or any committee I sit on or advise? | Go to A2 |
| A2 | Can the retainer be kept clearly separate from that matter: different decisions, different documents, different people where possible? | If no: decline or stop. If yes: write the separation into the advisory brief; declare where required |
| A3 | Do any of the three decisions in the advisory brief concern a submission, an application, or how a committee may respond? | Remove the decision or decline |
| A4 | Has the client asked, or do they appear to expect, access to or insight into a public body? | Correct it in writing; decline if the expectation remains |
| A5 | Is the client party to a matter where I have a decision-making or advisory role in any capacity? | Declare to the relevant secretariat; follow their direction; decline if in doubt |

### Part B: my other roles

| # | Question | If yes |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | Am I negotiating with this organisation on behalf of UNSW at the moment, or likely to in the term? | Declare to both sides before going further; decline if either objects |

| B5 | Do my roles require a declaration of this engagement or fee? | Lodge it; record the date |

### Quarterly re-run: the short form

At each quarterly review, ask the client directly and record the answers:

1. Has any product, technology or service of yours moved towards an application to a government advisory committee since the last check?
2. Have you entered any new negotiation, partnership or dispute I should know about?
3. Has anything changed in what you want from this retainer?

And ask myself: have my own roles, employment or other clients changed in a way that affects Parts A to C?

## 3. What I will stop and say if a conflict arises

Conflicts arrive mid-conversation. The rule is to stop first, then explain, then record.

### In a session or a call

**Trigger:** the conversation moves to a specific product's application, a submission, how a committee may respond, or anything about a body I sit on.

**What I do:** stop the conversation at that point. I do not hear the client out first, because once I have heard it I cannot unhear it.

**What I say:**

> "I need to stop you there. This is moving into an area I cannot advise on: it concerns [an application / a submission / a body I hold an appointment with]. I will not take it further, and I would ask you not to tell me more about it. I can carry on with [the general question / the next item]. I will note in this month's advisory note that I stepped back from this."

### When a document arrives that I should not review

**Trigger:** the review request form's confirmations cannot be ticked, or the document turns out to be part of a submission or to be promotional.

**What I do:** stop reading as soon as I realise. Delete my copy. Reply the same day.

**What I say:**

> "I have stopped reading this document and deleted my copy. It [forms part of a submission / is promotional material / contains information identifying individuals], which is outside what I can review. It has not counted against your reviews for the month. If there is a separate document on your general engagement approach, I am glad to review that."

### When the client's circumstances change

**Trigger:** the client tells me, or I learn from public sources, that a product is moving towards an application to a committee I sit on or advise, or that they have entered a negotiation with UNSW or another party I act for.

**What I do:** pause the retainer work that could be affected, that day. Re-run the standing check within 5 working days. Decide: carry on with a written separation, reshape the advisory brief, or end.

**What I say:**

> "Thank you for telling me. This may create a conflict for me, so I am pausing work on [decision or topic] today while I check. I will come back to you by [date] with one of three outcomes: I carry on with a clear written separation, I change the scope, or I end the retainer. If I end it, you pay only for work done to that date."

### When someone asks for access or inside knowledge

**What I say:**

> "I cannot help with that, and I would not if I could. My government advisory appointments are held independently of this practice. Clients receive no access to those bodies or their deliberations. What I can do is explain how the process works from what is public."

### When I realise I have a conflict the client does not know about

**What I do:** tell the client that a conflict has arisen and what I am doing about it, without disclosing another party's confidential information. Declare to any body that requires it.

**What I say:**

> "Something has changed on my side that affects my ability to advise you on [topic]. I cannot give you the detail because it is not mine to share. From today I will not advise on [topic]. If that removes too much of the value of the retainer, I will release you from the remaining term."

### Afterwards, every time

- [ ] Record in the conflict log: date, trigger, what I stopped, what I said, what was decided.
- [ ] Note it in that month's advisory note, in general terms.
- [ ] Lodge or update declarations with the relevant secretariat where required. Date: [date].
- [ ] If the retainer ends: refund any fee paid in advance for unused time; return or delete documents within 30 days.
- [ ] Review whether the fit test or the review request form needs tightening.

When in doubt, I step back. The cost of a perceived conflict is larger than the fee from any one retainer.

## 4. Confidentiality between clients

With no more than two retainers and one project at a time, clients may know or work with each other. These rules hold whether or not they do.

1. **I do not name clients.** No client is told who my other clients are, unless the other client has consented in writing to being named.
2. **Nothing crosses.** Documents, plans, figures, people's views and the content of sessions are used only for the client they came from. This continues after the retainer ends.
3. **What I may carry across:** general method and my own judgement, improved by experience. What I may not carry: anything that could be traced to a client, including "another organisation I work with did this".
4. **Separate records.** Each client has its own folder, notes, time log and conflict log. Nothing is filed in a shared place.
5. **No client material in general AI tools** where it identifies the client or any person. [Kris: confirm the tools and settings used.]
6. **If two clients' interests meet** (a partnership, a joint bid, a dispute): I tell each that a conflict has arisen, without saying with whom unless both agree, and I step back from that matter for both. I do not act as go-between.
7. **Benchmark use.** CEOF Diagnostic results are used in benchmark reporting only in de-identified form and with consent. A retainer client's results are never shown to another client.
8. **Consumers.** I do not pass consumers' names or details between clients, or from a client's engagement work to anyone else.
9. **Public content.** I do not write or speak about a client's situation in a way they or others could recognise, without written consent. I do not post about committee work at all.
10. **When it ends.** Client documents are returned or deleted within 30 days. My own advisory notes and time log are kept for [period, lawyer to confirm] and then deleted.

**If I breach any of these:** I tell the affected client promptly and plainly, say what happened and what I have done about it, and record it.

## 5. Boundaries of the role

| I do | I do not |
|---|---|
| Advise on the decisions in the advisory brief | Make the client's decisions |
| Review and comment on documents | Write the client's documents |
| Say what I think, including when it is unwelcome | Represent or speak for the client |
| Explain how the Australian system works from public sources | Advise on how a committee is likely to respond |
| Advise on how to involve and pay consumers properly | Recruit, contact or hold data about the client's consumers |
| Recommend stopping when the retainer is not working | Keep a retainer running because it is paid for |
| Declare the engagement where my roles require it | Use my public roles to win or keep the work |
