## 3. The 30-day follow-up call: agenda (60 minutes, online)

Internal. Have open: the summary note, the commitments table and the needs table.

| Minutes | Item | Notes for Kris |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | Welcome. Purpose: what happened when the plans met real life. Same ground rules as the day. | No recording. |
| 5 to 30 | Round: each project, two minutes on the three questions, then one minute of response from Kris or a colleague. | Keep time. Record status per project: run, booked, changed, stalled. For "stalled", ask what one thing would move it. |
| 30 to 42 | The common obstacle. Pick the barrier raised most often and work it as a group: what has anyone tried, what would work here. | Usual candidates: no approved payment line; recruitment only reaching known people; the decision was made before the session; no time to close the loop. |
| 42 to 50 | The organisation's side. Sponsor reports on the needs table: what has been done, what has not. | Brief the sponsor beforehand. If a need was not met, ask for a new date, not a reason. |
| 50 to 57 | Next commitments: each person names one thing for the next 30 days. Suggest pairs to check in with each other. | Record them for the close-out email. |
| 57 to 60 | Close. What the group does from here without Kris. | Finish on time. |

**Questions to draw on in the round**

- Did the level you chose hold, or did it move once you started?
- Who said yes to your invitation, and who did not? What does that tell you?
- Was everyone paid, at the rate you planned, on time?
- What happened in the session that the plan did not expect?
- Have participants heard back yet? What did they say?

**If a session went badly:** thank the person for saying so, ask what they noticed first, and ask the group what they would have done. Do not diagnose it for them.

**If few sessions have run:** that is normal at 30 days. Shift the round to "what is booked, and what would get it booked".
