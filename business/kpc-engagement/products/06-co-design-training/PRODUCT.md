# In-house co-design training: product file

Internal. The whole product: what is sold, how the day runs, and how Kris's two days are spent. Follow it as written so every organisation gets the same training.

Price: $9,500, excluding GST, fixed. One day, up to 12 people, with a workbook and a 30-day follow-up call. Kris's time: 2 days. Source of truth: `../../BRAIN.md`, section 4. Charity rate: 25% off for organisations with annual revenue under $1 million, limited to two a quarter. Proposal: `../../assets/proposal-template.md`.

All material in this folder is original to Kris Pierce Consulting. It reuses nothing created in any employment role and nothing owned by any charity. It describes the IAP2 Spectrum of Public Participation, a published framework, in Kris's own words with attribution.

## 1. The promise

By 4:30 every participant leaves with a one-page plan for a real session with community members on a real project of their own, and the skills to run it fairly and safely. Thirty days later the group meets again to say what happened.

## 2. Who it is for

- Organisations that involve, or are about to involve, consumers, carers or community members in decisions: patient organisations, research teams, trial networks, companies' patient engagement and medical teams, government programs.
- Teams where engagement happens but depends on one or two people, or where a CEOF Diagnostic showed a low score in Engagement Practice, Capability and Culture, or Safety, Ethics and Recognition.
- Not for: organisations with no upcoming project to work on. The day is built around one. Without it, postpone.

## 3. Learning outcomes

By the end of the day, each participant can:

1. Explain what co-design is and is not, and place any activity on the IAP2 spectrum: inform, consult, involve, collaborate, empower.
2. Name the decision a project needs, and choose the level of involvement that decision warrants, with a reason.
3. Write a recruitment plan that reaches beyond the people already known to the organisation.
4. Cost participation properly: payment at published rates, expenses and access, as a separate budget line.
5. Plan and run a session that is safe: informed consent, the right to withdraw, a plan for distress, care with stories.
6. Turn what people said into options and a decision, and show which input changed what.
7. Close the loop: tell participants what happened to their input, including what was not taken up and why.
8. Complete a one-page session plan for their own project.

## 5. What is excluded

- Venue, catering, and Kris's travel and accommodation (at cost, agreed in writing in advance).
- Reviewing or running participants' actual sessions. That is a retainer or a Consumer Engagement Design project.
- Formal accreditation or assessment. Participants receive the workbook and the summary note, not a qualification.
- Product-specific work on any application before a committee Kris sits on or advises. If a participant's project is tied to one, a different project is chosen before the day.
- Medical, legal or ethics-committee advice. The day teaches when to seek ethics review, not how a particular committee will rule.

**Optional, costed separately:** a community member as co-presenter for the afternoon sessions, or a small panel of two or three community members for a 30-minute conversation. ## 6. Who should be in the room

- 6 to 12 people. Below six, the peer work is thin; above 12, not everyone gets feedback.
- People who will plan or run engagement in the next six months: program and project leads, researchers and trial coordinators, engagement and communications staff, clinicians who lead projects.
- At least one person who controls a budget, because payment cannot be planned without one.
- The sponsor, for the first 20 minutes and the last 30 at least.
- Two or three people may share one project. No more than six projects in the room.
- If the organisation has community members in standing roles (an advisory group chair, a consumer board member), invite one or two as full participants, paid for the day.

## 8. Scoping call checklist (30 minutes)

- [ ] Why now: what prompted the training, and what should be different in three months?
- [ ] Who attends: names, roles, experience of engagement so far.
- [ ] The projects: up to six real, upcoming projects where community involvement is planned or should be.
- [ ] Conflict check on the projects: none tied to an application before a committee Kris sits on or advises.
- [ ] What exists already: a payment policy, an advisory group, a consent form, an ethics pathway.
- [ ] What has gone wrong before, if anything.
- [ ] Access needs of participants; dietary needs go to the client's organiser.
- [ ] Date, venue, start and finish; date of the 30-day call set now.
- [ ] Whether a paid community co-presenter or panel is wanted.
- [ ] Who receives the pre-work and sends reminders.

## 9. Room and materials

**Room**

- Seats 12 at three or four tables in groups (cabaret style), with space to walk between and clear wall space for flip chart sheets.
- Step-free access, an accessible toilet nearby, natural light if possible, a door that closes.
- Screen or projector with HDMI, and sound if online participants join.
- A quiet space nearby for anyone who needs a break.
- Tea, coffee and water from 8:45; morning tea 10:45; lunch 12:00; afternoon tea 2:30. Arranged by the client.

## 12. What it leads to

Training is usually bought after a debrief or a sprint, and sometimes before either. Offer a next step only if the 30-day call shows a need:

- Several participants stuck on the same organisational barrier (no payment policy, no accountable person): suggest the CEOF Diagnostic if not done, then a Diagnostic Debrief.
- One project with a hard decision attached: a Strategic Advisory Sprint.
- A team that wants someone to review plans as they go: the Ongoing Strategic Advisor retainer.
- Otherwise: nothing. Ask for written consent to name the organisation and for a referral.

## 13. Risks and how each is handled

| Risk | Handling |
|---|---|
| Participants arrive without a project | Pre-work asks for one; the sponsor supplies a list at scoping. Anyone without one joins a colleague's project. |
| The day turns into theory | Every session ends with participants writing in their own canvas. The run sheet caps Kris's input at 15 minutes per session. |
| A participant's project is tied to a committee Kris sits on or advises | Caught at scoping and again in pre-work. A different project is chosen. |
| Staff share distressing experiences of their own (many are also carers or patients) | Ground rules at 9:10; the quiet space; Kris models the distress response taught in session 4. |
| Senior people dominate | Table groups mixed by Kris from the pre-work; written work before discussion. |
| The organisation has no payment budget | Not a reason to skip the session. Participants cost it anyway; the sponsor hears the total in the closing session. |
| Fewer than six attend | Run it. Use the spare time for one-to-one review of session plans. |
| Sector examples do not fit | Step 2 tailors examples to research, industry, charity or government. |
| The 30-day call is poorly attended | Date fixed at scoping and written on the last page of the workbook; reminder in the summary note. |
