# Strategic Advisory Sprint: options appraisal template

> **What this is:** the working table Kris uses in week 3 to compare the options against criteria, before writing sections 3 and 4 of `decision-paper-template.md`. It takes its inputs from `interview-synthesis-grid.md` and the kickoff constraints.
> **Time:** inside the 3 hours for building options in week 3 (`sprint-plan.md`).
> **What it is for:** testing my own judgement, and showing the decision owner how I reached the recommendation if asked. The scores do not make the decision. If the table and my judgement disagree, I work out why before writing a word of the paper.
> **Rules:** two or three real options plus "do nothing". No straw options. Internal working document; only the plain-words summary and the trade-off table go into the paper.

Sprint: [client: organisation], [sprint type]. Decision: [the decision, as a question]. Date: [date].

Test for each: could a reasonable leadership team choose it? If not, it is a straw option. Remove it.

## Step 2. The criteria and their weights

Five or six criteria, no more. Take them from what the decision owner said a good answer must do (kickoff, minutes 42 to 50) and from the implications column of the synthesis grid. Weights add to 100.

| # | Criterion | What a high score means | Source | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Serves the decision by the date | Can be in place, or clearly under way, by [date] | Kickoff | [for example 25] |
| 2 | What it does for the community | Consumers and community members would see a real difference, in their terms | Consumer interviews | [for example 25] |
| 3 | Affordable in money | Within the stated ceiling of $[amount], with consumer payments at published rates included | Kickoff | [for example 15] |
| 4 | Affordable in people and time | Can be carried by named people without something else failing | Interviews | [for example 15] |
| 5 | Independence and trust | Protects the organisation's independence and the community's trust; for a company, sits comfortably inside the Medicines Australia Code of Conduct | Documents, interviews | [for example 10] |
| 6 | Lasts beyond year one | Survives a change of staff or funding | Interviews | [for example 10] |
| | **Total** | | | **100** |

Replace or reword criteria to suit the sprint type. Two rules: one criterion must always be about the community, and its weight is never the lowest. Cutting consumer payment rates is never a way to score higher on cost.

## Step 4. Three checks on the result

1. **Is it close?** If the top two totals are within 0.3 of each other, the table has not separated them. The choice turns on one trade-off; name it.
2. **Does it survive a change of weights?** Swap the two heaviest weights, then set all weights equal. Does the same option still lead? [yes / no. If no, which criterion decides it: (criterion).]
3. **Is there a veto?** Does any option score 1 on a criterion the decision owner said was not negotiable? If so it is out, whatever its total. [option, criterion, or "none"]

Then one question the table cannot answer: who would object to the leading option, and are they right?

## Step 5. Plain-words summary

Write this before the paper. If it cannot be written, the analysis is not finished.

**The choice is really between** [X] **and** [Y]. **It is a choice about** [the trade-off: for example, speed against reach, or control against independence].

**I recommend option [letter]: [name], because** [reason one, tied to a finding], [reason two], **and** [reason three].

**What you give up by choosing it:** [one sentence].

**Another option becomes the better choice if** [one or two conditions].

**Doing nothing means** [what continues, and what it costs by (date)].

**What the table did not capture:** [one sentence, or "nothing material"].

## Step 6. What carries forward into the decision paper

| From here | To the paper |
|---|---|
| Step 1, options | Section 3, column headings and "what it is" |
| Step 3, reasons for scores | Section 3 rows: what it achieves, what it costs, what you give up, main risk |
| Step 4, question on who would object | Section 3 row "who would object, and why"; section 6, risks |
| Step 5, "the choice is really between" | The two or three sentences under the section 3 table; slide 6 heading |
| Step 5, recommendation and three reasons | Section 1 one-sentence recommendation; section 4 |
| Step 5, "another option becomes the better choice if" | Section 4, "what would change my recommendation" |

The numbers stay in this file. The paper shows the trade-offs in words, because a leadership team argues with a score and decides on a trade-off. If the decision owner asks to see the table, share it with the weights explained.

Call the decision owner with the step 5 summary before the draft lands. No surprises in the room.
