# Strategic Advisory Sprint: product file

Internal. The whole product: what is sold, the four sprint types, how each is delivered and how the five days are spent. Follow it as written so every sprint runs the same way.

Price: $12,000, excluding GST, fixed. Kris's time: 5 days over four weeks. One decision. Source of truth: `../../BRAIN.md`, section 4. Charity rate: 25% off for organisations with annual revenue under $1 million, limited to two a quarter. A Diagnostic Debrief fee ($1,500) is credited if the sprint is booked within 60 days of the debrief.

The proposal for every sprint is written from `../../assets/proposal-template.md`, which carries the scope, fee table, consumer payments line, conflict check and terms. Do not duplicate those here. This folder holds the delivery kit.

## 1. The promise

Four weeks on one decision. At the end the leadership team has a decision paper of no more than eight pages with a clear recommendation, has been briefed on it, and can decide.

"If the work doesn't change a decision, it isn't done." A sprint that ends without the decision owner able to decide has not been delivered.

## 2. Who it is for

- An organisation with one named decision about consumer or community involvement, a named decision owner, and a date by which it must be made.
- Typical buyers: a patient organisation chief executive or board chair; a chief investigator or research manager; a patient engagement, medical affairs or market access lead in a company; a program director in government.
- Usually reached after a Diagnostic Debrief, where page 6 of the action plan named the decision.

Not a fit:

- Several decisions at once, or no decision yet. Offer a debrief first, or scope a Consumer Engagement Design project.
- The client wants someone to run engagement for them. That is Consumer Engagement Design.
- Anything tied to an application before a committee Kris sits on or advises.

## 3. What is included

| Included | Detail |
|---|---|
| Kickoff | 60 minutes with the decision owner and day-to-day contact |
| Document review | Up to 15 documents or about 150 pages, supplied by the client |
| Interviews | Up to six, 45 minutes each, online |
| Mid-point update | One written update at the end of week 2 |
| Decision paper | Up to eight pages; one round of consolidated comments |
| Leadership briefing | 45 minutes, with a ten-slide deck |
| Handover | Final paper, slides, and a first-30-days list |
| 60-day follow-up | One email, one reply |

## 4. What is excluded

- More than six interviews, workshops, surveys or co-design sessions. These belong to Consumer Engagement Design.
- Carrying out the recommendation. That is a retainer or a project, scoped separately.
- Consumer payments. Where any of the six interviews are with consumers or community members, they are paid at published rates and costed as a separate line in the proposal (section 7 of the proposal template). They are not part of the $12,000.
- Product-specific work on any application that is, or is likely to be, before a committee Kris sits on or advises: no drafting, reviewing or coaching on a submission, and no advice on how a committee is likely to respond.
- Medical, legal or regulatory advice.
- Travel. Sprints run online unless agreed; travel is at cost, agreed in writing in advance.

## 5. The four sprint types

Every sprint is one of these four. The type is chosen in the proposal and named in the kickoff. If the work does not fit one of them, it is not a sprint.

### 5.1 Consumer evidence plan

| | |
|---|---|
| The decision it serves | What evidence of consumer experience and preference should this organisation gather, by what methods, and what will it cost to do properly? |
| Who typically asks | A company building general consumer evidence capability; a patient organisation deciding what to collect about its community; a research team planning the consumer evidence stream of a program |
| Typical inputs | Any existing surveys, registries, advisory group records and patient-reported data; the organisation's evidence or research plan; public guidance on consumer input, read from public sources; interviews with the evidence owner, two or three internal users of the evidence, and two or three consumers or community organisation leads |
| The specific output | A decision paper that sets out the evidence questions worth answering, two or three method options with cost, time and burden on participants, a recommended plan with a 12-month sequence, a consumer payment budget as a separate line, and governance for how the evidence is held and used |
| Guardrail | The plan covers methods and capability. It is never tied to a live or likely application to a committee Kris sits on or advises. If the client's purpose is a specific submission, decline or reshape. |

### 5.2 Engagement strategy

| | |
|---|---|
| The decision it serves | How should this organisation involve its community over the next two to three years, who is accountable, and what will it fund? |
| Who typically asks | A patient organisation board; a research institute or trial network; a government program |
| Typical inputs | CEOF Diagnostic results and debrief action plan if there is one; strategic plan; current engagement activity and budget; terms of reference for any advisory groups; payment policy if any; interviews with the chief executive or program lead, a board member, two staff and two community members |
| The specific output | A decision paper with the organisation's position across the seven CEOF domains, two or three strategic options (for example: consolidate, build a standing structure, share decision-making in one program first), a recommended option with roles, budget and a 12-month sequence, and the three measures the board will see |
| Guardrail | Strategy, not delivery. The paper ends at the decision and the first 30 days. |

### 5.3 Trial-readiness review

| | |
|---|---|
| The decision it serves | Is consumer involvement in this trial or research program ready to stand up to funders, ethics reviewers and participants, and what must change before [date]? |
| Who typically asks | A chief investigator or research manager before a grant application or ethics submission; a trial network; a company planning a study that needs credible consumer involvement |
| Typical inputs | Draft protocol or program outline (consumer involvement sections); the funder's published consumer involvement requirements; participant-facing materials in draft; budget lines for involvement; any consumer panel records; interviews with the investigator, trial coordinator, two or three consumers or carers with relevant experience, and a site or recruitment lead |
| The specific output | A decision paper with a readiness rating against a short set of criteria (involvement in design, burden on participants, materials people can read, payment and recognition, feedback of results), the gaps that would be visible to a reviewer, options to close them with cost and time, a recommended fix list in order, and consumer payment costs for the budget |
| Guardrail | The review covers consumer involvement in research design and conduct. It does not cover a reimbursement or listing application, and it is not scientific or regulatory advice. |

### 5.4 Partnership approach

| | |
|---|---|
| The decision it serves | Should this organisation enter this partnership (or this kind of partnership), on what terms, and with what protections for its community and its independence? |
| Who typically asks | A patient organisation approached by a company or research group; a company or institute that wants to work with patient organisations properly; a government program choosing community partners |
| Typical inputs | The approach or draft agreement, if one exists; the organisation's policies on funding, conflicts and data; past partnership records; for company clients, their Code obligations as they state them; interviews with the decision owner, a board member or senior sponsor, the relationship lead, and two or three people from the other side's perspective (not the counterparty in a live negotiation unless both sides agree) |
| The specific output | A decision paper with what each side wants and can offer, two or three partnership models with trade-offs (for example: arm's-length funding, a defined joint project, a standing advisory relationship), a recommendation with terms to insist on and terms to refuse, a disclosure and independence checklist, and a first-30-days approach plan |
| Guardrail | For company clients the Medicines Australia Code clause in the proposal applies. No access to any public body is offered or implied. If Kris is negotiating with either party on behalf of an employer, declare to both sides before proposing. |

## 7. What the client prepares

- One decision, written as a question, with a decision owner and a date. Agreed in the proposal, confirmed at kickoff.
- One day-to-day contact with authority to book people and release documents.
- Documents within five working days of kickoff (list in `kickoff-agenda.md`).
- Up to six interviewees, named at kickoff and booked by the client's contact within week 1.
- The leadership team's time for a 45-minute briefing in week 4, booked at kickoff.
- One round of consolidated comments on the draft, within three working days.
- Where consumers are interviewed: agreement on the published rate schedule used, and who pays them.

## 8. Quality bar

The paper is not sent until every line is true.

- [ ] The decision on page 1 is word for word the decision in the proposal, or the change was agreed in writing.
- [ ] Eight pages or fewer, including the out-of-scope page. No appendices beyond a one-page list of sources.
- [ ] The recommendation is stated in the first half page and can be said aloud in one sentence.
- [ ] At least two real options are set out, plus "do nothing", each with trade-offs. No straw options.
- [ ] Every finding can be traced to a document or to at least two interviews. Single-source points are marked as such.
- [ ] No interviewee is identifiable without their agreement.
- [ ] "What it takes" gives people, money and time as figures or ranges, with consumer payments as a separate line.
- [ ] The first 30 days has owners and dates the decision owner has seen.
- [ ] The out-of-scope section is filled in.
- [ ] No reference to Kris's public appointments, no product claims, nothing tied to a live application.
- [ ] No em dashes, no exclamation marks, no banned words, no "we" for Kris.
- [ ] The decision owner has seen the recommendation before the briefing. No surprises in the room.

## 9. What happens after a sprint

A sprint ends with a decision, and sometimes that is the end of the work. The follow-on is offered once, in the handover email and again at 60 days, and only where it fits:

| If the decision leads to | The fitting next step | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| A period of carrying out the recommendation where judgement is needed month to month | Ongoing Strategic Advisor, $3,500 or $6,500 a month, six-month minimum | `../../BRAIN.md` |
| A defined build: sessions with consumers, a framework to run | Consumer Engagement Design, $18,000 to $45,000 | `../../BRAIN.md` |
| Staff who need the skills | In-house co-design training, $9,500 | `../06-co-design-training/` |
| Nothing further | Say so. Ask for consent to be named, and for a referral if the work was useful | |

Capacity rule from the brain: no more than two retainers and one project at a time. Check before offering.

## 10. Risks and how each is handled

| Risk | Handling |
|---|---|
| The decision changes mid-sprint | The proposal's assumption applies: a change is agreed in writing and may change fee and dates. Raise it at the mid-point update, not in the paper. |
| Interviewees cannot be booked in week 1 | The client's contact books them; Kris supplies the invitation in `emails.md`. Any interview not held by the end of week 2 is dropped, and the paper says so. |
| Documents arrive late | Review what has arrived by day 5 of week 1. Later documents are read only if they bear on the options. |
| The leadership team wants a different answer | The decision owner sees the recommendation before the briefing. Disagreement is recorded in the paper's risks, not edited out. |
| Scope grows into delivery | "Out of scope" is the last section of the paper. Use it, and name the offer that covers the extra work. |
| A conflict emerges (a product moving towards a committee Kris sits on or advises) | Section 9 of the proposal applies: change the scope or stop; the client pays only for work done. |
| Consumers interviewed without payment or support | Not permitted. Payment is confirmed before the invitation is sent; the interview guide has the consent and support steps. |
| Five days is exceeded | The hours in `sprint-plan.md` are caps. The first thing to cut is interview count, then paper length. Never the briefing. |
| Confidentiality between interviewees and leadership | Stated at the start of every interview: points are reported without names. |
