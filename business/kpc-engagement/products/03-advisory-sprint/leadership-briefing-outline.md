# Strategic Advisory Sprint: leadership briefing outline

Internal. The 45-minute briefing in week 4 and the ten slides that go with it. The briefing exists to get the decision made. It is not a presentation of the work.

## 1. Before the briefing

- [ ] The decision owner has heard the recommendation and seen the final paper.
- [ ] Pre-read sent two working days ahead: the decision paper and one line, "Please read pages 1 and 5 at least."
- [ ] The decision owner has agreed to open and close the session.
- [ ] Kris knows who in the room is likely to object, and to which option.
- [ ] Slides built from the paper. Nothing on a slide that is not in the paper.

## 2. The 45 minutes

| Minutes | Part | Who leads | Slides |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Why this is on the agenda, and what is being asked of the room today | Decision owner | 1 |
| 3 to 6 | The decision and the recommendation, stated up front | Kris | 2 |
| 6 to 14 | What I found, including what your community said | Kris | 3, 4, 5 |
| 14 to 20 | The options and the trade-off between them | Kris | 6 |
| 20 to 25 | What it takes and the main risks | Kris | 7, 8 |
| 25 to 40 | Discussion | Decision owner chairs; Kris answers | 9 on screen |
| 40 to 45 | The decision, or the date it will be made; first 30 days; close | Decision owner | 10 |

Kris speaks for no more than 22 minutes in total. The other 23 belong to the room.

## 3. The ten slides

One idea per slide. A heading that states the point, and no more than 30 words of body text or one table.

| # | Slide | Heading states | Content | Paper section |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | The decision, as a question | Organisation, date, "Decision paper briefing", decision owner's name | 1 |
| 2 | The recommendation | "I recommend [option], because [reason]" | The one-sentence recommendation; the three things the room is asked to decide | 1, 4 |
| 3 | How I got here | "[number] documents, [number] interviews, four weeks" | Sources by type and role, no names; what was out of scope in one line | 1, 8 |
| 4 | What I found | The single most important finding | The three findings that drive the recommendation, one line each | 2 |
| 5 | What your community said | A plain statement of what consumers and community members told me | Three points in their terms; one quotation only if agreed | 2 |
| 6 | The options | "This is a choice between [X] and [Y]" | The options table cut down to four rows: what it is, cost, what you give up, main risk | 3 |
| 7 | What it takes | "[$] and [people] in year one" | People, money, time; consumer payments shown as their own line; what stops to make room | 5 |
| 8 | Risks | The risk most likely to undo this | Top three risks and what reduces each | 6 |
| 9 | Questions for the room | "Three questions to settle today" | The three decisions from slide 2, restated as questions. Stays on screen through discussion | 1 |
| 10 | The first 30 days | "If you decide today, this happens by [date]" | Four or five actions with owners; the first action this week | 7 |

If time is short when building: slides 3, 8 and 9 can be dropped, leaving seven.

## 4. Lines to have ready

- Opening: "You have the paper. I will take 20 minutes on what matters for the decision and leave the rest for you."
- When asked for detail not in the paper: "That was outside the scope of this sprint. It is listed on page 8, and I can tell you who is best placed to answer it."
- When the room prefers another option: "That is a reasonable choice. Page 5 sets out when it becomes the better one. What it costs you is [trade-off]."
- When the discussion drifts into delivery: "That is a question for the first 30 days. Can I check whether the decision itself is settled?"
- When a decision cannot be made in the room: "What would you need to see to decide, who will bring it, and by what date?"
- If asked about committee processes or a specific application: "I do not advise on anything before a committee I sit on or advise, so I will leave that there."

## 5. Discussion prompts for the decision owner (minutes 25 to 40)

1. Does anyone read the findings differently?
2. Which option would you choose if money were not the constraint? Which if time were not?
3. What would our community say about the option preferred in this room?
4. Who owns this from tomorrow?
5. Are we deciding today? If not, what is missing?

## 6. After the briefing (inside the handover time)

- Record the outcome in one line: decided, decided with changes, or deferred to [date] pending [what].
- Send the handover email the same day or the next (`emails.md`, 4).
- Draft the summary for the people interviewed, for the client's contact to send.
- Update the pipeline tracker and diarise the 60-day follow-up.

## 7. Variations

- **Board, not executive:** shorten Kris's part to 15 minutes; the chair leads discussion; send the pre-read with the board pack.
- **In person:** same timings. Print slide 6 and the "what it takes" table as a single handout.
- **Decision owner alone:** run slides 2, 6, 7 and 10 as a conversation in 30 minutes.
