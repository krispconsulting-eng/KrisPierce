# Strategic Advisory Sprint: kickoff agenda

> **What this is:** the 60-minute kickoff, with the script points for Kris and the checklist that must be complete before the call ends. The agenda table can be pasted into the calendar invitation.
> **Who attends:** the decision owner, the day-to-day contact, and no more than two others. Online.

## Agenda (60 minutes)

| Minutes | Item | What is settled |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | Welcome and how the four weeks run | Everyone knows the four milestones: interviews, mid-point update, draft paper, briefing |
| 5 to 15 | The decision | The decision, as one question. Who decides. By when. What happens if no decision is made |
| 15 to 25 | What is already known | What has been tried, what the organisation already believes the answer is, and where people disagree |
| 25 to 35 | Who to hear from | Up to six interviewees, named, with the reason each is on the list |
| 35 to 42 | Documents | The list, who sends them, by when |
| 42 to 50 | What a good answer looks like | The constraints: budget ceiling, people, dates, things that are not negotiable. What would make the paper unusable |
| 50 to 55 | Boundaries | What is out of scope; confidentiality of interviews; consumer payments; conflicts |
| 55 to 60 | Dates and next steps | All dates confirmed; the first action for each side |

## Script points

**0 to 5. Opening.**
"This sprint serves one decision. In four weeks you will have a paper of no more than eight pages with a recommendation, and I will brief your leadership team on it. My job is to make the decision easier to make, not to make it for you."

**5 to 15. The decision.** Read the decision from the proposal aloud. Then ask:

1. Is this still the decision, in these words?
2. Who makes it: one person, an executive, a board? Who can stop it?
3. What is the date, and what is driving the date?
4. What happens if nothing is decided?
5. Is there a preferred answer already? (If so, say it will be tested as one of the options.)

If the decision has changed, stop and agree the new wording now. A change of substance is confirmed in writing after the call.

**15 to 25. What is already known.**

1. What have you already tried, and what happened?
2. Where do people inside the organisation disagree about this?
3. What do you think your community would say?
4. What would you rather I did not spend time rediscovering?

**25 to 35. Who to hear from.** Aim for a spread, and name the reason for each:

| Slot | Who | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The decision owner | Constraints and appetite |
| 2 | The person who will carry out whatever is decided | Practicality |
| 3 | Someone who holds the money or the risk (finance, board member, compliance) | What it must satisfy |
| 4 and 5 | Consumers, carers or community organisation leads | What it means for the people affected. Paid at published rates |
| 6 | A sceptic, inside or outside | The strongest argument against |

Adjust by sprint type using the lists in `interview-guide.md`.

**35 to 42. Documents.** Ask for no more than 15. Standard list:

- [ ] The strategic or program plan the decision sits within
- [ ] CEOF Diagnostic report and debrief action plan, if there are any
- [ ] Current engagement activity: groups, terms of reference, recent consultation reports
- [ ] Payment or recognition policy for community members, if any
- [ ] Budget lines relevant to the decision
- [ ] Any earlier paper, review or proposal on this decision
- [ ] By sprint type: evidence or research plan (consumer evidence plan); board papers on engagement (engagement strategy); protocol sections on involvement, participant materials and the funder's published requirements (trial-readiness review); the approach, draft terms and funding and conflict policies (partnership approach)

Say plainly: no documents that identify individual patients or family members, and nothing from a submission to a committee.

**42 to 50. What a good answer looks like.**

1. What is the most you could spend, and the most staff time you could give?
2. What is fixed and cannot change?
3. Who has to be comfortable with the answer for it to hold?
4. What would make you put the paper in a drawer?

**50 to 55. Boundaries.** Cover all four, briefly:

- Scope: read the out-of-scope lines from the proposal.
- Interviews: "What people tell me is reported without names. I will not attribute a view to a person without their agreement."
- Consumer payments: confirm the rate schedule, who pays, and that payment is arranged before invitations go out.
- Conflicts: "If anything changes that could create a conflict, such as a product moving towards an application to a committee I sit on or advise, tell me straight away. My government advisory appointments are held independently of this practice, and this work gives no access to those bodies or their deliberations."

**55 to 60. Dates.** Fill the dates table in `sprint-plan.md` together. Confirm the briefing is in diaries.

## Before the call ends

- [ ] Decision wording confirmed, with owner and date
- [ ] Six interviewees named, with contact details to follow within two working days
- [ ] Client contact will send invitations (text supplied by Kris)
- [ ] Document list agreed, due date set
- [ ] Constraints recorded: budget, people, fixed points
- [ ] Consumer payment arrangements confirmed, or confirmed as not applicable
- [ ] Mid-point update date, draft date, comment date and briefing date agreed
- [ ] How Kris and the contact will communicate between milestones (email, and one call if needed)

## Straight after (within the kickoff hour allocation)

Send a five-line confirmation to the decision owner and contact: the decision as worded, the six names, the document due date, the dates, and anything that changed from the proposal.
