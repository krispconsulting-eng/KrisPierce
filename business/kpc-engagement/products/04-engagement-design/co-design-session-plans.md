# Co-design session plans

> **What this is:** three reusable 2-hour session plans. Standard and Full run all three. Focused runs sessions 1 and 3, using the variant at the end of session 1. Each plan works online or in person, for 4 to 8 participants.

## Rules for every session

- Two people run it: me as facilitator, and a second person for participant support (see the distress protocol in `consumer-recruitment-and-payment-kit.md`).
- Start with the spoken consent check and the group agreement.
- One break of at least 10 minutes.
- Finish on time. If an activity runs over, cut the next one, not the break or the close.
- No product names. No clinical advice. For company clients, no company staff in the room.
- Notes are written up within 2 working days against the raw input log in `synthesis-method.md`.

**Group agreement (read out, then ask what to add):**
1. Share what you choose. No one has to tell their story.
2. One person at a time.
3. Disagree with ideas, not with people.
4. What is said here stays here.
5. Pass at any time. Step out at any time.

## Session 1: Understanding experience

**Purpose:** understand what it is like to deal with [organisation or program] from the consumer's side, and where involvement has mattered or been missing. Output feeds CEOF domains Voice and Representation, Engagement Practice, Safety, Ethics and Recognition, and Feedback and Learning.

**Question the session answers:** when have you been asked, when have you been heard, and what happened next?

| Time | Activity | How |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:10 | Welcome | Who I am, who is paying, what this is for, what is and is not open to influence. Spoken consent check. Group agreement. |
| 0:10 to 0:25 | Introductions | Name, where you are joining from, and one thing you hope changes because of these sessions. No diagnosis stories required. |
| 0:25 to 0:50 | Activity 1: Moments that mattered | Individually, 5 minutes: note one time an organisation asked for your view and it went well, and one time it did not. Then share one of them. I capture each on a card under "what made it work" or "what got in the way". |
| 0:50 to 1:00 | Break | |
| 1:00 to 1:30 | Activity 2: The path of a decision | I show one real decision from the decision inventory as a simple line: idea, planning, doing, reporting. In pairs or small groups: where on this line should people like you have been involved, and how? Mark the line. Report back. |
| 1:30 to 1:50 | Activity 3: What it costs to take part | Round: what does taking part cost you (time, money, energy, risk), and what would make it worth it? Cover payment, timing, format, safety, and hearing back. |
| 1:50 to 2:00 | Close | I read back the three things I heard most clearly and ask what I missed. What happens next, when payment arrives, how to add anything afterwards. One-word check-out. |

**Materials:** decision line (one slide or one sheet); cards or sticky notes in two colours; the "what is and is not open to influence" list; support contacts; payment details sheet.

**Online notes:** use one shared board with the decision line and two columns; I type for anyone who prefers to speak. Pairs go to breakout rooms for Activity 2 only; the second person stays in the main room and is reachable by private chat. Cameras optional. Phone dial-in available. Send the two Activity 1 prompts in advance so people can think.

**In-person notes:** tables of four; the decision line drawn on a wall sheet at reachable height; quiet room available; the second person seated near the door. Food on arrival, not during the opening.

**Notes to capture:** each moment as a separate line; where on the decision line each mark sat; every cost named; exact phrases worth quoting (with a flag for quote consent).

### Focused variant (session 1 with priorities)

Focused has no session 2, so session 1 carries a short priority exercise. Change the timings as follows:

| Time | Change |
|---|---|
| 0:25 to 0:45 | Activity 1 shortened to 20 minutes |
| 0:45 to 0:55 | Break |
| 0:55 to 1:20 | Activity 2 shortened to 25 minutes |
| 1:20 to 1:30 | Activity 3 as a quick round: one cost, one thing that would make it worth it |
| 1:30 to 1:50 | Priority exercise: I read back the issues on the cards, grouped. Each person has three votes for "fix this first". Then one question: is there something with few votes that still must not be lost? |
| 1:50 to 2:00 | Close |

## Session 2: Setting priorities

**Purpose:** move from what was heard to what matters most, and decide the level of involvement consumers want in each decision type. Output feeds Purpose and Governance, Voice and Representation, and Outcomes and Impact.

**Question the session answers:** of everything raised, what should [organisation] fix first, and how much say should consumers have in each kind of decision?

**Before the session:** I send a one-page summary of session 1 themes, in plain language, 3 working days ahead.

| Time | Activity | How |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:10 | Welcome back | Consent check. Group agreement reminder. What I did with session 1. |
| 0:10 to 0:25 | Check the themes | I present 6 to 10 themes from session 1. Is this what you said? What is missing or wrong? Themes are corrected on the spot. |
| 0:25 to 0:55 | Activity 1: What first | Each person places three votes on the themes that matter most. Discuss the top results: why these? Then: which low-vote theme must not be dropped, and who is affected if it is? Low-vote items that someone defends are marked "protect". |
| 0:55 to 1:05 | Break | |
| 1:05 to 1:40 | Activity 2: How much say | I introduce five levels of involvement in plain words: told, asked, worked with, deciding together, deciding. (These are the IAP2 levels: inform, consult, involve, collaborate, empower.) For each decision type in the inventory, the group places it at the level it should sit at, and marks where it sits now. Gaps of two levels or more are discussed. |
| 1:40 to 1:52 | Activity 3: What good looks like | One question, taken from CEOF domain 7: what outcome would tell you [organisation] is doing its job? Each person offers one. |
| 1:52 to 2:00 | Close | Read back the top priorities, the protected items and the biggest gaps. Next steps, payment, check-out. |

**Materials:** theme cards from session 1; voting dots (three each); a grid with decision types down the side and the five levels across the top; two marker colours for "now" and "should be".

**Online notes:** voting by dots or stamps on the shared board, or by private message to me for anyone who prefers; the grid is one board everyone can see while I move the markers as directed. Keep the whole group together for Activity 2; breakouts lose the argument that matters.

**In-person notes:** themes on wall sheets with space between them; the grid as one large sheet on a table people can stand around or reach from a chair; votes placed during a quiet 5 minutes so no one follows the loudest voice.

**Notes to capture:** vote counts per theme; every "protect" item with the reason given and who it affects; for each decision type, the "now" and "should be" level and any disagreement; each outcome statement word for word.

**For research clients:** where the client uses the Consumer Engagement Framework v1.0, the decision types on the grid are its five research phases: Priority Setting; Research Design; Conduct and Data Collection; Analysis and Interpretation; Dissemination and Translation.

## Session 3: Testing the draft approach

**Purpose:** put the draft approach in front of the people who shaped it and find where it breaks. Output feeds all seven domains, with weight on Engagement Practice and Feedback and Learning.

**Question the session answers:** would this work for you and for people who are not in this room, and what must change before it is adopted?

**Before the session:** I send the draft approach on two pages, 3 working days ahead: levels of involvement by decision type; the calendar; payment and recognition; how people hear back. I also send what the organisation has said it cannot do, and why (from decision point 2).

| Time | Activity | How |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:10 | Welcome back | Consent check. What has happened since last time, including what [organisation] said yes and no to, and why. |
| 0:10 to 0:30 | Walk through the draft | I present the draft in four parts, 5 minutes each. Questions of clarification only. |
| 0:30 to 0:55 | Activity 1: Keep, change, drop | For each of the four parts, each person marks keep, change or drop, with one line of reason. Discuss the "change" and "drop" marks first. |
| 0:55 to 1:05 | Break | |
| 1:05 to 1:30 | Activity 2: Who would this fail | Think of someone in your community who is not here: someone newly diagnosed, someone in a remote area, someone who does not use English at home, someone who has had a bad experience with [organisation]. Where does this draft fail them? What would fix it? |
| 1:30 to 1:45 | Activity 3: The no list | I read out what [organisation] has said it will not do, and why. Is the reason clear? Is it fair? What would you want said differently in the report back? The answers do not reverse the decision; they shape how it is explained and are recorded. |
| 1:45 to 1:55 | Staying involved | How would you want to hear what happens? Who would like to be told about future opportunities? (Opt in only.) |
| 1:55 to 2:00 | Close | What I will take back. When the written report back arrives. Final payment. Thanks. |

**Materials:** the two-page draft, printed or on screen; a keep, change, drop sheet for each of the four parts; the "no list" with reasons; opt-in form for future contact.

**Online notes:** share the draft section by section, not all at once; keep, change, drop as a poll for each part, then open discussion; Activity 2 works well in breakouts of three with one persona each.

**In-person notes:** four stations, one per part of the draft; people move in pairs for 6 minutes at each, then gather; the "no list" is read aloud, not handed around.

**Notes to capture:** keep, change, drop counts and reasons for each part; every failure point named in Activity 2 and the fix proposed; reactions to each item on the no list; who opted in to future contact (stored separately from session notes).

## After every session

- [ ] Thank-you message within 24 hours (see `emails.md`).
- [ ] Payment triggered within 2 working days.
- [ ] Notes written up within 2 working days and entered in the raw input log.
- [ ] 10-minute debrief with the second person: what worked, who to follow up, what to change next time.
- [ ] Anything a participant asked to withdraw is removed.

## Full size: running two groups

Run each session plan twice, with the same materials and questions. Keep the groups separate through all three sessions so each builds trust. Where the brief calls for it, form the groups around a real difference (for example, people living with the condition, and parents and carers). In synthesis, findings are tagged by group and differences are reported, not merged.
