# Consumer Engagement Design: product definition

> **What this is:** the internal definition of the Consumer Engagement Design project, so it is quoted and delivered the same way every time. Client-facing wording is marked. Prices are AUD, excluding GST, as set in `../../BRAIN.md` section 4.
> **Status:** draft for Kris to confirm. Nothing here is published or sent without Kris's approval.

## 1. Promise (client-facing)

In 6 to 12 weeks you will have an engagement framework your organisation can run without me: who you involve, in which decisions, at what level, how they are paid, how they are kept safe, and how you report back. It is built with consumers in the room, and it is tied to the decisions you have to make in the next year.

If the work doesn't change a decision, it isn't done.

## 2. Who it is for

| Segment | Typical trigger | Usual size |
|---|---|---|
| Research institutes, trial networks, MRFF and NHMRC applicants | A grant or program that requires consumer involvement reviewers will believe | Focused or Standard, costed into the grant budget |
| Patient organisations and rare disease charities | A funder or partner asking how the community shapes decisions | Focused or Standard |
| Government and health agencies | A program needing a designed or reviewed engagement approach | Standard or Full |
| Biopharma, biotech and healthtech in rare disease | Building an engagement function that stands up inside the Medicines Australia Code of Conduct | Standard or Full, with the company conditions in section 9 |

Best entry point: a completed CEOF Diagnostic and a Diagnostic Debrief, so the three lowest domains are already known. It can be bought without them; in that case the diagnostic is completed in week 1.

Not for: an organisation that wants a consultation report to file, a team with no named decision owner, or a company wanting consumer input on a specific product application (see section 6).

## 4. What is included in every size

- One named decision the work serves, written into the proposal.
- A conflict check before acceptance, recorded in the proposal (see `../../assets/proposal-template.md`, section 9).
- One round of consolidated comments on the draft framework.
- A CEOF Diagnostic at the start if none exists, and a recommendation to re-assess at 12 months.
- Every participant receives the report back before the client receives the final framework, or on the same day.

## 5. Consumer payments (always a separate line)

Consumers and community members are paid for their time at a published rate. The payment is never taken out of my fee and never offered as a voucher in place of payment unless the participant asks for one.

Default schedule: Health Consumers Queensland, Remuneration and Reimbursement of Consumers Position Statement (rates from 1 July 2024). Detail, alternatives and the caveat on checking the primary source are in `consumer-recruitment-and-payment-kit.md`.

Example participant cost lines at the default schedule, before expenses:

Participant expenses (travel, care costs, access needs) are added at cost. Interpreters, captioning and venue hire are also outside my fee and are quoted at cost.

## 6. What is excluded

- Product-specific work on any application that is, or is likely to be, before a committee I sit on or advise. No drafting, reviewing or coaching on a sponsor's submission, and no advice on how a committee is likely to respond.
- Consumer input gathered for use in a specific product application.
- Research requiring ethics approval. This is engagement and service design. If the client intends to publish findings as research, the client obtains ethics approval and the timeline changes.
- Running the framework after handover. That is the client's job, or an Ongoing Strategic Advisor retainer.
- Staff training beyond the handover session (the in-house co-design training day is a separate offer).
- Extra sessions, extra participants or extra interviews beyond the size bought. These move the client to the next size.
- Medical, legal or regulatory advice. The consent and payment wording is supplied as a draft for the client's legal review.
- Design and typesetting of the framework beyond a clean document in the client's template or mine.

## 7. Client responsibilities

- Name one decision owner and one day-to-day contact with authority to decide.
- Supply documents and interview availability within 5 working days of the request.
- Approve the recruitment brief and participant pack within 5 working days, including legal review of the consent wording.
- Agree the payment schedule and who pays participants before recruitment opens. Pay participants within 14 days of each session.
- Circulate the expression of interest through the organisation's own channels, or agree a partner organisation to do so.
- Fund access needs: interpreters, captioning, travel, care costs.
- Have the leadership team attend the handover session.
- Commit, in writing, to sending the report back to participants.

## 8. Quality bar

The work is finished when all of these are true:

1. Every recommendation in the framework traces to a row in the findings table.
2. Every decision type in scope has a level of involvement, a named owner and a date.
3. Minority views are recorded and answered, not averaged away.
4. Participants have been paid and have received the report back.
5. The "what we will not do and why" section is filled in honestly.
6. A staff member who was not in the project can read the framework and run the first quarter.
7. The measures use the seven CEOF domain names exactly and have a baseline or a date to set one.
8. Nothing in the deliverables identifies an individual participant without their written agreement.

## 9. Company clients: conditions that apply every time

For pharmaceutical, biotech and medical technology companies, the whole project sits inside the Medicines Australia Code of Conduct. Lawyer to confirm wording against the current edition before first use.

- No product promotion. No product names in session materials, questions or deliverables. Topics are the condition, the experience of care, and how the company engages, not a product.
- Consumer contact is at arm's length. Recruitment runs through me or an independent patient organisation. Company staff do not attend consumer sessions and do not receive names, contact details, recordings or transcripts.
- No individual data to the company. Findings are reported as themes. Quotes are used only with written agreement and only where they cannot identify a person. Counts are not reported for groups smaller than ten.
- Participants are paid through Kris Pierce Consulting at cost with no margin, or through the independent patient organisation, so the company holds no individual payment records. [Kris: confirm with accountant how these pass-through payments are handled.]
- Participants are told who is funding the work, in plain words, before they agree to take part.
- Nothing is directed at encouraging anyone to ask for a prescription medicine.
- The framework covers the company's general engagement capability. It is not tied to a live or likely application.

## 10. Risks and how they are handled

| Risk | Early sign | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Recruitment falls short | Fewer expressions of interest than twice the places by the closing date | Extend by one week once; widen channels; run with a minimum of 4 per group and say so in the findings |
| The same familiar voices apply | Applicants all known to the client | Selection against the brief's diversity criteria; reserve half the places for people new to the organisation |
| A participant becomes distressed | Any session | Distress protocol in the recruitment and payment kit; second person on every session |
| Leadership will not accept a finding | Pushback at the midpoint check | Record it in "what we will not do and why"; do not soften the finding |
| Scope grows | New decisions or groups added after kickoff | Written change: move to the next size or park the item in the first-year plan |
| Payment delays to participants | Client finance process over 14 days | Agree the payment route before recruitment; I pay at cost and invoice if the client cannot |
| Conflict arises mid-project | A product moves towards an application | Stop, say so, reshape or end; the client pays for work done to date (proposal template, section 9) |
| Client treats the framework as finished | No owner named for the first quarter | Handover is not booked until section 5 of the framework (roles) is filled in |

## 11. What it leads to

- **CEOF Diagnostic re-assessment at 12 months**, with a Diagnostic Debrief ($1,500).
- With written consent, a named case for the service page. No client is named without it.
