> **What this is:** Kris's guide for a 30-minute discovery call: purpose, five questions, one-sentence offer descriptions, qualifying and disqualifying signs (including conflict triggers), and next-step scripts.
> **Where it goes:** internal only. Keep it open during calls booked from the website, the diagnostic emails or outreach. Not for publication.

---

## Purpose

Leave the call knowing three things:

1. The one decision this organisation needs help with, and when it is due.
2. Whether I am the right person for it, and whether any conflict rules it out.
3. The next step, agreed out loud before the call ends.

This is not a pitch. If the honest answer is "you do not need me", say so.

## Before the call (5 minutes)

- Read their CEOF Diagnostic report if they have one.
- Check the organisation against the conflict triggers below.
- Open pipeline-tracker.csv and add the row.

## Running order

| Minutes | What |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Open. "Thank you for making the time. I would like to understand the decision in front of you, then tell you plainly whether I can help. Does that suit?" |
| 3 to 18 | The five questions. Listen more than talk. |
| 18 to 24 | Say back what I heard, name the decision, and describe the one offer that fits. |
| 24 to 28 | Scope limits, independence line if relevant, price. |
| 28 to 30 | Agree the next step and the date. |

## The five questions

1. **"What decision are you trying to make, and who makes it?"**
   Listening for: a real decision with an owner. If there is none, the work has nothing to change.

2. **"What happens if nothing changes in the next six months?"**
   Listening for: a deadline, a funder, a grant round, a launch, a review. No consequence usually means no budget.

3. **"How are consumers involved in your work today, and what has that changed?"**
   Listening for: the gap between activity and influence. If they have a diagnostic report, use it: "Your seven domain results suggest [observation]. Does that match what you see?"

4. **"Who else needs to agree, and what would they need to see?"**
   Listening for: the real approver, internal resistance, and whether a leadership briefing is needed.

5. **"What have you set aside for this, in time and budget?"**
   Listening for: a figure or a range. If there is none, state the price of the offer that fits and watch the reaction.

## Each offer in one sentence

- **CEOF Diagnostic (free):** "A free online diagnostic that shows where your consumer engagement stands across seven domains, with a report straight away."
- **Diagnostic Debrief ($1,500):** "Ninety minutes with me on your results, then a written plan within five working days: three priorities, what to stop, and what to do in the next 90 days."
- **Strategic Advisory Sprint (from $12,000):** "Four weeks on one decision, ending with a decision paper and a briefing to your leadership team."
- **Consumer Engagement Design ($18,000 to $45,000):** "I design and deliver the engagement with you, including co-design sessions with paid consumers, and leave you with a framework you can run yourselves."
- **Stakeholder Alignment Program ($15,000 to $30,000):** "I bring sponsor, clinicians and community organisations to a shared position before a program or launch."
- **Ongoing Strategic Advisor ($3,500 or $6,500 a month, six-month minimum):** "A monthly working session, document review and advice on call, for as long as it is useful."
- **In-house co-design training ($9,500):** "One day for up to 12 of your people, with a workbook and a follow-up call 30 days later."
- **Speaking ($3,500; charities by arrangement):** "A keynote or panel contribution prepared to your brief."

All prices exclude GST. The debrief fee is credited against a sprint booked within 60 days. Charity rate: 25% off the sprint and training for organisations with revenue under $1 million, two places a quarter.

## Qualifying signs

- A named decision with an owner and a date.
- A deadline set by someone else: a grant round, a funder report, a program start, a market entry.
- The person on the call can approve the spend, or can name who does.
- They talk about what consumers changed, or admit that nothing changed.
- They accept that consumers are paid, as a separate line.
- They have completed the diagnostic, or agree to before the next conversation.
- Priority segments for the first 90 days: research teams writing 2027 grant applications, and biotech entering Australia without a local patient engagement lead.

## Disqualifying signs

- No decision at stake: they want engagement "done" so it can be reported.
- They want consumer endorsement of a decision already made.
- They expect consumers to take part unpaid.
- They want a named client list, a testimonial or a statistic I cannot honestly give.
- The budget is far below the offer that fits, and the charity rate does not apply or is full for the quarter.
- My capacity is full: two retainers and one project. Offer a later start date; do not add days.

## Conflict triggers (stop and check before going further)

- The organisation has a product, technology or service that is, or is likely to be, before MSAC, PBAC or any committee I sit on or advise, and the work touches it.
- They ask me to draft, review or coach on a submission.
- They ask how a committee is likely to respond, or what a committee is thinking.
- They mention my public roles as a reason for calling, or ask for an introduction to a public body.
- I am negotiating with the organisation on behalf of UNSW at the same time.
- The work would reuse material that belongs to UNSW or to SCN2A Australia.

**What to say when a trigger appears:**

"I need to stop you there. My government advisory appointments are held independently of this practice. Clients receive no access to those bodies or their deliberations. I do not work on product-specific submissions, and I cannot discuss how a committee might respond. What I can do is [general capability work, methods not tied to a live application]. If that is not what you need, I am not the right person, and I would sooner tell you now."

Then note the trigger in the tracker, and do not send a proposal until the conflict check is complete.

## Next-step scripts

**No diagnostic yet:**
"The most useful next step costs you nothing. Take the diagnostic this week; it gives you a report straight away. I will send the link today. Once you have your results, book a debrief or reply to me and I will tell you what I would look at first."

**Diagnostic done, decision not yet clear:**
"I would start with a debrief. Ninety minutes on your results, then a written plan within five working days. It is $1,500 plus GST, and if you go on to a sprint within 60 days the fee is credited. I will send the booking link today. Which week suits?"

**Clear decision with a deadline:**
"This fits a sprint: four weeks on that one decision, ending with a decision paper and a briefing to your leadership team. It is from $12,000 plus GST. I will send a proposal by [day], after I have completed my conflict check. Who else should receive it?"

**Larger design or alignment work:**
"This is bigger than a sprint. I will send a proposal by [day] with a fixed scope and fee, and with consumer payments shown as a separate line. To scope it properly I need [documents or names] from you by [day]."

**Retainer:**
"A retained arrangement suits this: a monthly working session, document review and advice on call. It is $3,500 a month for a day and a half, or $6,500 for three days, with a six-month minimum. I would suggest starting with [tier]. Shall I send the agreement?"

**Not a fit:**
"I do not think I am the right person for this, and I would sooner say so now. [One sentence on why.] If it helps, the diagnostic is free and the report is yours to use."

## After the call (5 minutes)

- Update pipeline-tracker.csv: stage, offer, value, next action and date.
- Send the promised link or a two-line summary the same day, signed "Best regards, Kris".
- If a proposal is due, complete the conflict checklist in proposal-template.md first.
