## Week 1, post 1: there is now a standard

On 7 July 2026 the Framework for consumer engagement in HTA was published. (Source: the Australian Government's health.gov.au page on HTA improvements.)

For years, "consumers were engaged" was a sentence nobody could test. Now there is a published framework to read it against.

That changes the question organisations get asked. It used to be "did you involve consumers?" It is becoming "show me how they shaped the decision".

Most organisations I speak with can answer the first question. Very few can answer the second with a document they would be comfortable handing over.

If you had to show it tomorrow, what would you hand over?

## Week 1, post 2: collected is not the same as used

One figure has stayed with me this year.

IQVIA reported that 66% of Australian drug HTA submissions in 2024 included consumer input, against an average of 44% across its benchmark markets. The same brief found that the evidence was underused. (Source: IQVIA Management Consulting insight brief, April 2026, "Valuing the patient voice in medicines access decisions". It is IQVIA's own analysis.)

Read that as two findings. Australia is ahead on collecting consumer input. Collecting it has not been enough.

Input becomes evidence when it is gathered against a question a decision-maker has to answer, by a method they can inspect. Otherwise it is a well-meant attachment.

The test I apply to my own work: if it doesn't change a decision, it isn't done.

## Week 2, post 4: added too late

The most common problem I see in engagement plans has nothing to do with method.

It is timing. Consumers are invited in after the question has been set, the design has been agreed and the budget has been spent. At that point the only thing left for them to do is react.

People can tell when they are being asked to react. So can anyone who later reads the plan.

The fix is unglamorous. Before the work starts, write down which decisions are still open and bring consumers in while they are. If no decision is open, be honest that you are informing people, and call it that.

Engagement that starts early is cheaper, and it is the only kind that can change anything.

## Week 3, post 6: pay consumers, and show it

A small test of whether an engagement plan is serious: find the line in the budget that pays the consumers.

If it is missing, the plan depends on people donating their time to an organisation that is paying everyone else in the room.

If it is buried inside a consultant's fee, nobody can see what participants receive.

In every proposal I write, consumer payments are a separate line, at published rates, apart from my own fee. The client can see exactly what goes to the people taking part, and so can the people taking part.

It takes one row in a table. It tells consumers, funders and reviewers more about your intentions than any values statement.

## Week 4, post 7: what to stop

When I work through an organisation's engagement with its leaders, the most useful part is often the list of things to stop.

The annual survey nobody reads. The advisory group that meets quarterly and is never asked a real question. The consultation that runs after the decision is made.

Each one costs staff time and consumer goodwill, and each one makes the next request harder. People remember being asked for input that went nowhere.

Stopping is harder than starting, because every activity has an owner and a history. It is also where the time for better work comes from.

If you listed everything your organisation does in the name of engagement, which items could you connect to a decision that changed?

## Week 4, post 8: 2027 is close

The Strategic Agreement between the Commonwealth and Medicines Australia runs from 2022 to 2027. (Source: Medicines Australia fact sheet, "Enhanced Consumer Engagement".)

Put that beside the Framework for consumer engagement in HTA, published on 7 July 2026 (source: health.gov.au), and the direction is plain. Expectations of how organisations involve consumers are being written down.

Capability of this kind is slow to build. It rests on relationships with communities, methods people trust and staff who know how to use what they hear. None of that can be assembled in the month before it is needed.

If consumer engagement is on your 2027 plan, the useful work is in the next two quarters.
