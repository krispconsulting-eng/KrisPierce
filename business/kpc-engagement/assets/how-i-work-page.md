## Who this is for

I work with organisations that make decisions about rare disease research, evidence and access.

- **Patient organisations and rare disease charities** that need to show funders and partners their engagement is real, and want to be ready for research and industry partnerships.
- **Research institutes, trial networks and grant applicants** that need to meet consumer involvement requirements in a way reviewers believe.
- **Biopharma, biotech and healthtech teams in rare disease** that are building an engagement function and consumer evidence methods that stand up, inside the Medicines Australia Code of Conduct.
- **Government and health agencies** designing or reviewing a consumer engagement approach.

You work with me directly. I am not an agency and I do not hand the work to a team.

## The path

Most clients move through four steps. You can stop at any of them, and you can start at whichever one fits.

**1. Diagnostic.** You complete the CEOF Diagnostic online. It is free, and you receive a summary report across seven domains without speaking to anyone.

**2. Debrief.** You and I work through your results for 90 minutes. Within five working days you have a written action plan.

**3. Sprint.** Four weeks on the one decision that matters most. It ends with a decision paper and a briefing to your leadership team.

**4. Ongoing.** Where it is useful, I stay on as a retained adviser.

My standard for all of it: if the work doesn't change a decision, it isn't done.

## What is out of scope

I do not take on product-specific work for any application that is, or is likely to be, before a committee I sit on or advise. That means I do not draft, review or coach on a sponsor's submission, and I do not advise on how a committee is likely to respond.

What I do take on: explaining how the Australian system works from public sources, building your organisation's general engagement capability, designing consumer evidence methods that are not tied to a live application, and work for patient organisations, researchers and government.

Every proposal I send has been through a conflict check first. If a conflict exists, I will tell you and decline or reshape the work.

## Questions

**Why do you publish prices?**
Because you should know what a piece of work costs before you ask for a conversation. Each offer has a fixed scope, so the price can be stated. Where a range is shown, the proposal fixes the figure before you commit.

**Do I have to take the diagnostic first?**
No. It is the quickest way to see where you stand, and it gives the first conversation something specific to work from. If you already know the decision you need help with, book a call and start there.

**What happens to my diagnostic answers?**
They are used to produce your report. [Kris: confirm against the privacy policy before publishing: answers are added to benchmark reporting only in de-identified form and only with consent.]

**Can you help with our submission to PBAC or MSAC?**
No. I do not work on product-specific submissions or advise on how a committee is likely to respond. I can help you build the engagement capability and consumer evidence methods that sit behind good work, as long as they are not tied to a live application.

**How are consumers paid in an engagement project?**
At published rates, in every project. Consumer payments are costed and shown as a separate line in the proposal, so you can see exactly what goes to the people who take part.

**Do you work with pharmaceutical and biotech companies?**
Yes, under a written agreement, at fair market value for the work done, and within the Medicines Australia Code of Conduct. The same out-of-scope rule applies to every company client.
