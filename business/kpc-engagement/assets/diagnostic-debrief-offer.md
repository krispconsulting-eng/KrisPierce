## (d) Structure of the written action plan

Up to six pages, delivered within five working days of the session. One page per heading. Plain language, no appendices.

**Cover block (top of page 1, not a separate page)**
Organisation, date of the debrief, who attended, and one sentence naming the decision or deadline this plan serves.

**Page 1. Where you stand**
- A one-paragraph reading of the seven domain results taken together.
- The two strongest domains and what they make possible.
- The two weakest domains and what they put at risk.
- Anything said in the session that changes how the results should be read.

**Page 2. Priority one**
- The priority, stated as an outcome.
- Why it comes first: the decision or obligation it affects.
- What done looks like, in terms someone outside the organisation could check.
- Who owns it and what it will take (time, people, money).

**Page 3. Priority two**
Same four points as priority one.

**Page 4. Priority three**
Same four points as priority one.

**Page 5. What to stop**
- Up to three activities to stop or pause, each with the reason.
- What stopping frees up (time, budget, goodwill).
- Who needs to be told, and how.

**Page 6. The next 90 days**
- A table: action, owner, date, and the evidence that will show it is done. Grouped as days 1 to 30, 31 to 60 and 61 to 90.
- The first action to take this week.
- A short closing note: what I would do next if this were my organisation, and whether a Strategic Advisory Sprint is the right step or not. If it is, the note names the one decision the sprint would serve and reminds the client that the debrief fee is credited within 60 days.

**Standing footer on every page**
