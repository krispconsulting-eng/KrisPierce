## What you get

- Your overall level, on a five-level scale: Ad hoc, Emerging, Structured, Embedded, Community-led.
- A result for each of the seven domains:
  1. Purpose and Governance
  2. Voice and Representation
  3. Capability and Culture
  4. Engagement Practice
  5. Safety, Ethics and Recognition
  6. Feedback and Learning
  7. Outcomes and Impact
- Your three priorities, drawn from your three lowest domains, with a first move for each.
- A written report, sent to the email address you give.

---

## How long it takes

About ten minutes. There are about 28 statements across the seven domains. Answer for your organisation as it is now, not as it plans to be. You can complete it alone or with one or two colleagues in the room.

- Your answers are stored to produce your report.
- They are used in de-identified form for a sector benchmark only if you give consent below. The benchmark never names an organisation.
- They are never sold.
- I use your email address to send your report and to follow up about it. You can ask me to delete your answers at any time: info@krispierce.com.au.

## Result page

### Header (all levels)

**Overline:** Your result

**Heading:** Your organisation is at Level [number]: [level name].

**Line under the heading:** Your written report is on its way to [email]. Below is your overall level, your seven domain results, and the three domains to work on first.

**Section labels:**
- Your overall level
- Your seven domains
- Your three priorities
- First move (label for the move shown under each priority)

**Note under the priorities:** These are your three lowest domains. Start with one move in each. I suggest taking the diagnostic again in 12 months.

### Level 1: Ad hoc

Engagement in your organisation happens when someone thinks of it. That is a starting line, not a verdict. Your three priorities below each come with one first move; choose the one you could start this month.

### Level 2: Emerging

There is genuine engagement activity in your organisation, and it is not yet connected to decisions. The effort is real; the next step is to tie it to something that is actually being decided. Your three priorities show where that connection is weakest.

### Level 3: Structured

You have reliable ways of involving consumers, and their involvement does not yet shape your plans. The mechanisms are in place, which is the harder part to build. Your priorities point to where input should start changing what you do.

### Level 4: Embedded

Community involvement is part of how your organisation makes decisions. Your priorities show the domains that lag the rest, and the work now is to keep the standard consistent across all seven.

### Level 5: Community-led

Your community shares power over direction, priorities and resources. The task at this level is to keep it that way as people and programs change. Your priorities show where to look first, and a repeat diagnostic in 12 months will show whether it is holding.

### First moves by domain (shown under each priority; wording from the CEOF reference)

| Domain | First move |
|---|---|
| Purpose and Governance | Take a one-page proposal to the next board meeting: why the organisation involves its community, who is accountable, and what it needs. |
| Voice and Representation | Stand up one small advisory group with clear terms of reference and one real decision on its agenda. |
| Capability and Culture | Build a simple induction for community members and a one-session briefing for staff. |
| Engagement Practice | Add one question to project planning: what decision is this, and what involvement does it warrant? |
| Safety, Ethics and Recognition | Adopt and publish a recognition and payment policy for community members, even a modest one. |
| Feedback and Learning | Close one loop this month: go back to the last group consulted and tell them what happened to their input. |
| Outcomes and Impact | Ask the community one question: what outcome would tell you we are doing our job? Start measuring it. |

Note for Kris: the last first move uses "we" inside a question the organisation asks its own community. It is the organisation speaking, not Kris, so it stays as written in the workflow.
