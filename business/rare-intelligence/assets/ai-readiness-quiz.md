# AI readiness quiz for rare disease organisations

**Intro copy for the quiz page**

Ten questions. About four minutes. An instant result and one suggested next step.

Answer for your organisation as it is today, not as you would like it to be. There are no wrong answers, and nothing you enter is shared. Please do not type any patient, family or member details into this quiz.

This quiz is a conversation starter, not an audit, and it is not legal or privacy advice.

## Scoring

Each question has three options. A scores 0, B scores 1, C scores 2. The total runs from 0 to 20.

## The questions

**1. How is AI being used in your organisation right now?**
- A. Not at all, as far as I know. (0)
- B. Some of us use it individually; we have never discussed it as a team. (1)
- C. We use it for agreed tasks and have talked about how. (2)

**2. Do you have a written AI policy or guideline?**
- A. No. (0)
- B. Informal rules, or a draft that has not been adopted. (1)
- C. Yes, adopted by the board or leadership and shared with staff and volunteers. (2)

**3. Does everyone who might use AI know what information must never go into a general AI tool?**
- A. We have not talked about it. (0)
- B. Most people would guess correctly, though it is not written down. (1)
- C. Yes. It is written down, and identifiable patient and family information is clearly ruled out. (2)

**4. Which AI accounts do people use for the organisation's work?**
- A. Personal accounts, or I do not know. (0)
- B. A mix of personal and organisational accounts. (1)
- C. Accounts the organisation controls, with the settings checked. (2)

**5. When AI helps produce something, who checks it before it goes out?**
- A. Whoever wrote it, if they have time. (0)
- B. It is usually read by someone, though there is no set step. (1)
- C. A named person checks facts, figures and references against the source every time. (2)

**6. Can you name one task where AI would clearly help your organisation?**
- A. Not yet. (0)
- B. I have a few ideas and have not tested them. (1)
- C. Yes, and we have tried it on real work. (2)

**7. Is any AI workflow written down so a colleague could run it?**
- A. No. (0)
- B. One person has a way of doing it, in their head. (1)
- C. Yes, at least one workflow is documented and used by more than one person. (2)

**8. If a member, funder or journalist asked "do you use AI, and how?", could you answer?**
- A. I would not know what to say. (0)
- B. I could answer for myself, not for the organisation. (1)
- C. Yes. We have an agreed answer and say where AI has been used. (2)

**9. Has your board or committee discussed AI?**
- A. No. (0)
- B. It has come up in passing. (1)
- C. Yes. It has been on the agenda, and someone is responsible for it. (2)

**10. If someone pasted a member's personal information into an AI tool by mistake, what would happen?**
- A. I am not sure anyone would report it. (0)
- B. They would probably tell someone, and we would work it out. (1)
- C. We have written steps and a named person to tell. (2)

## Result bands

### 0 to 7: Starting out

**What this means.** AI is either not in use or is being used quietly, without shared rules. That is where most organisations are. In Infoxchange's 2025 sector survey, as cited by PwC Australia in May 2026, 67% of not-for-profits used generative AI and 14% had a policy or guideline.

**The risk at this stage.** Someone, with good intentions, puts a member's story or a spreadsheet of contacts into a general AI tool.

**Do this week.**
1. Tell your team one rule today: no identifiable patient or family information in general AI tools.
2. Ask who is already using AI and for what. No blame; you need the picture.

**Recommended next step.** Start free. Download the guide, *5 AI workflows any rare disease charity can build this week*, and come to the free monthly session, "AI for rare disease organisations: where to start and what to avoid".

**Buttons:** Get the guide. Save a place at the free session.

### 8 to 14: Using it, without firm rules

**What this means.** People are getting value from AI, and the organisation has not caught up. You have ideas and some good habits; they live in individuals, not in writing.

**The risk at this stage.** Uneven practice. One person checks everything; another sends a draft straight out. Your board cannot answer a funder's question about how you use AI.

**Do this week.**
1. Write your rules on one page and take them to your next team meeting.
2. Choose one workflow and name the person who checks its output.

**Recommended next step.** Two routes, depending on what you need first.
- If your board wants rules it can adopt: the **AI Safe-Use Policy Sprint**. Two weeks, an AI use policy adapted to your organisation, a data-handling rule sheet, a risk register and a two-hour staff session. $3,500.
- If you want to build with peers: the **Signal Circle**. One working AI workflow in your organisation by month three. $97 a month or $970 a year; founding rate $67 a month for the first 25 members, locked for life.

**Buttons:** See the Policy Sprint. See the Signal Circle.

### 15 to 20: Ready to build

**What this means.** You have rules, a checking habit and at least one workflow in use. You are ahead of most of the sector. The question now is where AI should go next, and how to make it last beyond the one person who set it up.

**The risk at this stage.** Reliance on a single enthusiast, and workflows that grow without anyone reviewing them.

**Do this week.**
1. Time one existing workflow, including the checking, and note what it saves and what it costs.
2. List the next three tasks worth building, in order.

**Recommended next step.**
- To decide where to go next: the **AI Readiness Consult**. 90 minutes on your organisation and a written one-page plan. $1,500.
