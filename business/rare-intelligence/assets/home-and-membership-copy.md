## The promise

By the end of your third month you will have at least one AI workflow running in your organisation: tested on your own material, written down so a colleague can run it, and covered by a short set of safe-use rules.

What that asks of you: about an hour a week, one real task to work on, and a willingness to show a rough draft to peers.

What it does not promise: a particular number of hours saved. Every organisation is different, so you time the task before and after and judge for yourself.

## Who it is for

- Chief executives, founders and managers of rare disease patient organisations and foundations.
- Operations, communications, fundraising and research leads in those organisations.
- Board members and volunteers who carry real operational work, including parent carers running an organisation around everything else.

## Who it is not for

- Individual patients and families looking for help with a diagnosis or treatment. The newsletter and guides are free for you, and nothing here is medical advice.
- Companies seeking help to market a product. Pharmaceutical and device companies can fund places for patient organisations (see Sponsored places) but do not join the community.
- People who want AI to run without a human check. Every workflow here keeps a person in charge.
- Organisations looking for custom software development. That is a different service.

## What is inside

### Ten modules

Six follow the SIGNAL method. Four apply it to the work rare disease organisations do every week.

**The SIGNAL method**

| # | Module | What you leave with |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Source:** choose the job and gather the right material | One workflow chosen, your own documents collected, and a clear line between what can and cannot go into an AI tool |
| 2 | **Interpret:** get a useful first draft | A reusable prompt that gives the AI your context, audience and house style |
| 3 | **Ground:** check AI's work | A checking routine for invented facts, invented references and overconfident claims, built on the finding that an AI reviewer does far better once expert criteria are written down |
| 4 | **Navigate:** rules, privacy and disclosure | A one-page set of safe-use rules for your team, and wording for telling members and funders how you use AI |
| 5 | **Act:** put the workflow into your week | A written procedure a colleague can follow, tried on a live task |
| 6 | **Learn:** review, measure and improve | A before-and-after timing, a list of what failed, and the next workflow chosen |

**The applied strands**

| # | Module | Workflows covered |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | **Research:** making sense of the science | Plain-language summaries of papers and trials for families; keeping a research watch list; preparing questions for researchers |
| 8 | **Advocacy:** submissions and the public voice | First drafts of consumer submissions from de-identified member input, with a human check for invented evidence; consultation responses; briefing notes |
| 9 | **Resources:** money and people | Grant sections drafted from your own past applications; funder reports; donor and volunteer communications |
| 10 | **Execution:** running the place | Meeting notes to actions; board papers; a month of social posts from one newsletter; handover notes |

## Questions, answered

**1. Is this medical advice?**
No. The Signal Circle teaches people who run organisations how to use AI in their work. It does not advise on diagnosis, treatment or care, and nothing in it replaces a clinician.

**2. Do I need to be technical?**
No. If you can write an email, you can do this. We use general AI assistants such as Claude or ChatGPT through their ordinary chat windows. No coding.

**3. Which AI tool do I need, and what does it cost?**
Any current general AI assistant will do for most workflows. Many have a free tier; paid plans are a separate cost from membership and vary by provider, so check current pricing. We stay tool-neutral and show the same workflow in more than one tool where we can.

**4. What about privacy and our members' stories?**
This is where we start, not where we finish. The first rule is simple: do not put identifiable patient or family information into general AI tools. Module 4 gives you a one-page rule sheet, and the AI Safe-Use Policy Sprint is there if your board wants a full policy.

**5. How much time does it take?**
Plan for about an hour a week: a short prompt, one live session a month, and time on your own workflow. The sessions are recorded if you miss one.

**6. Can my colleagues or board join?**
Yes. Extra seats for people in the same organisation are $47 a month each. Each seat gets full access.
