# PAGE 1: THE NEED

## Sponsored Circle places

### Help ten rare disease organisations put AI to work safely, with no strings attached.

*Rare Intelligence. AI for rare-disease leaders.*

Rare disease patient organisations are run by a handful of people doing the work of a much larger team. Many are led by parents and carers. They are being asked to respond to consultations, summarise research for families, report to funders and answer their community's questions, often in the evenings.

AI can take real weight off that work. It is also already in use, mostly without rules.

**What the evidence says**

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| 67% of Australian not-for-profits use generative AI | Infoxchange, Digital Technology in the Not-for-Profit Sector 2025, as cited by PwC Australia, May 2026 |
| 14% have an AI policy or guideline | Same source |
| 23% have a documented cyber security plan | Reported by IT Brief Australia from the same Infoxchange research |
| 54.8% of rare disease patients and caregivers surveyed had used generative AI in relation to their condition | Journal of Participatory Medicine, 2026; 115 respondents, recruited in the United States |
| 78.3% had not discussed AI-generated information with a clinician | Same study |
| 82% of respondents want to be told when a clinician uses AI | European Patients' Forum, AI Survey Report 2026; 874 responses |

**What that means**

Patient organisations sit between a community that is already asking AI about its condition and a health system that has not caught up. Their members ask them what to trust. Most have no position, no policy and no time to develop one.

General AI training for not-for-profits exists, and some of it is free. What we could not find is a program built for rare disease organisations, with their particular questions: very small populations where people are easily identified, health stories held in trust, evidence that must not be invented, and relationships with industry that must stay independent.

That is the gap Sponsored Circle places fill.

# PAGE 2: WHAT A SPONSOR FUNDS

## Ten organisations. Twelve months. One working workflow each by month three.

A sponsor funds a year of Signal Circle membership for ten patient organisations.

**The Signal Circle** is the Rare Intelligence membership for people who run rare disease organisations. Each sponsored organisation receives one full membership place for 12 months, which includes:

- **Ten modules.** Six follow the SIGNAL method (Source, Interpret, Ground, Navigate, Act, Learn). Four apply it to the work these organisations do: Research, Advocacy, Resources and Execution.
- **A live build session every month,** where one workflow is built from a blank page, and recorded.
- **Monthly office hours** for the things that are stuck.
- **The template library,** including a one-page staff rule sheet and an AI use policy outline.
- **A peer community** of people running similar organisations.

**The aim for each organisation:** at least one AI workflow in use by the end of month three, tested on its own material, written down so a colleague can run it, and covered by safe-use rules.

**Examples of workflows members build**

- A research paper turned into a plain-language summary for families, checked by a person with relevant expertise.
- A grant application section drafted from the organisation's own past applications.
- Meeting notes turned into decisions and actions.
- A first draft of a consumer submission from de-identified member input, with a human check for invented evidence.

**Safety comes first.** Every member is taught, before anything else, not to put identifiable patient or family information into general AI tools. Nothing in the program is medical advice. No real patient information is used in any session.

# PAGE 3: TERMS OF SUPPORT

## What the sponsor receives

- **Plain acknowledgement.** The sponsor is named as the funder of the cohort wherever the sponsored places are described: on the Rare Intelligence website, in the welcome to sponsored organisations and in the annual summary. Wording: "Ten Signal Circle places in [year] are funded by an unrestricted grant from [sponsor]. The sponsor has no say over content, speakers, participants or findings and receives no individual information."
- **Two aggregate reports,** at six and twelve months (see page 4).
- **A written agreement and an itemised tax invoice,** so the support can be recorded and publicly reported as the sponsor's own obligations require.
- **One briefing conversation** with Kris at the end of the year on what the cohort learned about AI use in patient organisations, in general terms.

## What the sponsor does not receive

- **No say over content.** The sponsor does not review, approve, suggest or receive in advance any module, session, template, speaker or communication.
- **No say over who takes part.** Rare Intelligence makes the final selection against published criteria (page 4).
- **No access to the community.** Sponsor staff do not join the Signal Circle, attend sessions or see discussions.
- **No individual data.** No names of participants, no contact details, no organisation-level results, no session recordings.
- **No introductions, meetings or endorsements.** Sponsorship does not buy access to patient organisations or to Kris's advice on any product or application.
- **No product presence.** No product names, product messages, advertising or promotional material appear anywhere in the program. Nothing in it is directed at encouraging anyone to ask for a prescription medicine.
- **No exclusivity** over a condition area, and no right to veto other sponsors.
- **No co-branding of course content.** The sponsor's name appears in acknowledgements only.

## Independence and disclosure

1. **Unrestricted and arm's length.** Support is provided as an unrestricted grant for the stated purpose. These terms are written into the agreement.
2. **Disclosed in plain words** wherever the supported activity appears, and to every sponsored organisation before it accepts a place.
3. **Rare Intelligence is a business, not a patient organisation.** The agreement says so plainly, so the support is described and reported correctly. 4. **Fair value.** The price is the published member price for ten annual places. Nothing else is supplied to the sponsor.
5. **No product-specific work.** Rare Intelligence does not draft, review or coach on any sponsor's regulatory or reimbursement submission, and sponsorship has no bearing on any other matter.
6. **Conflict check.** Before an agreement is signed, Kris checks the sponsor against current roles and declarations and may decline. Sponsorship is declared wherever Kris's other roles require it.
7. **Sponsored organisations stay free.** A sponsored organisation owes the sponsor nothing: no meeting, no acknowledgement of its own, no data. It may decline a place because of who the sponsor is.
8. **Either side can end it.** If the terms are breached, Rare Intelligence may end the arrangement; places already granted to organisations continue for the 12 months.

# PAGE 4: SELECTION, REPORTING AND NEXT STEP

## How organisations are selected

**Who can receive a place.** Patient organisations and foundations working in rare disease, with a named person who will take part. Priority goes to organisations that are small, volunteer-led or without paid staff, and to those that could not otherwise pay.

**Two routes.**

1. **Open application.** Organisations apply through a short form on the Rare Intelligence site. This is the default.
2. **Sponsor suggestions.** A sponsor may suggest organisations or a focus, such as a state, or organisations of a certain size. Suggestions are treated as applications. They carry no extra weight, and Rare Intelligence makes the final decision.

[Kris: BRAIN.md says a sponsor may name organisations; the guardrails say a sponsor has no say over participants. This draft resolves it as "may suggest, Rare Intelligence decides". Confirm.]

**Criteria, published in advance.**

- A rare disease patient organisation, or one very close to it.
- A named lead with time to take part: about an hour a week.
- Leadership support for adopting safe-use rules.
- Need: size, budget and current capacity.
- A spread of conditions, regions and organisation types across the cohort.

**Process.** Applications are assessed by Rare Intelligence. The sponsor is told how many organisations applied and how many were placed, and nothing else. Organisations are told who the sponsor is, and the terms, before they accept.

## Reporting: aggregate only

The sponsor receives two short reports, at six and twelve months. Each contains whole-cohort figures only:

- Places filled and still active.
- Sessions held and overall attendance.
- Modules completed across the cohort.
- The number of organisations reporting at least one working workflow, and the number reporting a written set of AI rules.
- Types of workflow built, as categories.
- Themes from anonymous feedback, in summary.

**Minimum cell size of ten.** No figure is reported for any group smaller than ten organisations. For a ten-place cohort that means whole-cohort totals only, with no breakdown by condition, state or size, and no figure at all if fewer than ten organisations contribute to it. No organisation is named, quoted or described in a way that could identify it, unless that organisation has itself chosen to speak publicly.

The same summary is published on the Rare Intelligence site, so the community sees what the sponsor sees.
