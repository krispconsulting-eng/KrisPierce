# The first eight weeks in the Signal Circle

## The aim

By the end of week 8, every active member has: a signed-off set of one-page AI rules; one workflow built, tested, timed and written down; and a colleague who has run it. That puts members a month ahead of the promise (one working workflow by month three) and leaves room for a slow start.

## How each week works

- **Monday:** the weekly prompt is posted. One question, answerable in five minutes.
- **Midweek:** the live session, in the weeks that have one. Monthly live build session and monthly office hours, as promised on the membership page, plus a one-off welcome call in week 1. Other weeks carry a short recorded walkthrough.
- **Any time:** the template for the week is released in the classroom.
- **By Friday:** the member action. One thing, small enough to do in under an hour.

House rules, pinned at the top of the community: no identifiable patient, family, member or donor information, here or in any general AI tool. No product promotion. Nothing here is medical, legal or privacy advice. What members share about their organisations stays here.

## Week 1: Start here

**Module:** 1. Source: choose the job and gather the right material (part one).

**Weekly prompt:** "Introduce yourself in three lines: your organisation, your role, and the one task that eats most of your week. No need to polish it."

**Live session:** Welcome call, 45 minutes. How the Circle works, the house rules, and a round of introductions. Kris shows how to choose a first workflow: frequent, low risk, built on material you already own, and easy to check.

**Template released:** The one-page staff rule sheet (blank, ready to adapt).

**Member action:** Choose one workflow to build over the eight weeks and post it in the "My workflow" thread. Read the rule sheet and note two things you would change for your organisation.

**Facilitator note:** Reply to every introduction within a day. Pair members with similar tasks.

## Week 2: What goes in, what stays out

**Module:** 1. Source (part two): green, amber and red information.

**Weekly prompt:** "Name one type of information in your organisation that must never go into a general AI tool, and one you were unsure about."

**Live session:** Live build session, 60 minutes: a published research paper into a plain-language summary for families. Built from a blank chat using a published paper only. Includes the first weak result and how to improve it.

**Template released:** Traffic-light sorting sheet, with the rare disease de-identification test.

**Member action:** List the material your chosen workflow needs and sort each item green, amber or red. If anything is red, post the question (without the detail) and we will find a way to work without it.

**Facilitator note:** The "unsure" answers are the teaching material for the session. Collect them beforehand.

## Week 3: A useful first draft

**Module:** 2. Interpret: get a useful first draft (part one).

**Weekly prompt:** "Share a prompt that gave you a disappointing result. Remove anything identifying first. We will fix a few together."

**Live session:** Office hours, 45 minutes. Bring your workflow and whatever is stuck.

**Template released:** The context block: four lines to put at the top of any request (who we are, who will read this, what good looks like, what to do when unsure).

**Member action:** Write your own context block and produce a first draft for your chosen workflow using green or amber material only. Post the prompt, not the output.

**Facilitator note:** Rewrite two or three members' prompts in the thread and explain each change.

## Week 4: Write it down

**Module:** 2. Interpret (part two): from a good prompt to a repeatable one.

**Weekly prompt:** "One month in. What has surprised you, and what has been harder than you expected?"

**Live session:** None this week. Recorded walkthrough, about 10 minutes: turning a one-off prompt into a workflow someone else can run.

**Template released:** The workflow canvas: task, input, prompt, checks, owner, catch.

**Member action:** Fill in the workflow canvas for your workflow. One page. Post a photo or copy.

**Facilitator note:** First check on the promise. Message anyone who has not chosen a workflow and offer a 15-minute call. [Kris: confirm whether one-to-one catch-up calls are on offer.]

## Week 5: Check AI's work

**Module:** 3. Ground: check AI's work (part one).

**Weekly prompt:** "Tell us about a time AI got something wrong for you: an invented fact, a wrong name, a figure from nowhere. What tipped you off?"

**Live session:** None this week. Recorded walkthrough, about 12 minutes: reading an AI summary against its source, and why written criteria make a review stronger than instinct.

**Template released:** The output checking routine: numbers, names, quotes, references, certainty, tone.

**Member action:** Take one output from your workflow and check it line by line against the source. Count what you corrected and post the count.

**Facilitator note:** Praise the members who found the most errors. Finding them is the skill.

## Week 6: The human check, built in

**Module:** 3. Ground (part two): reviewers and evidence.

**Weekly prompt:** "Who checks your AI-assisted work before it goes out? If the answer is 'me, when I have time', say so. Most of us start there."

**Live session:** Live build session, 60 minutes: a grant application section from your own past applications, with placeholders where a current figure is needed. Kris uses a fictional organisation's material.

**Template released:** The evidence check for submissions: every claim traces to member input or a source a person has read.

**Member action:** Name the person who reviews your workflow's output and add them to the canvas. Run the task once the old way and once with the workflow, and time both, including the checking.

**Facilitator note:** Ask members to share timings only if they wish, and never present them as a result others should expect.

## Week 7: Rules, privacy and disclosure

**Module:** 4. Navigate: rules, privacy and disclosure (part one).

**Weekly prompt:** "If a member or funder asked 'do you use AI, and how?', what would you say today? Draft two sentences."

**Live session:** Office hours, 45 minutes, with a focus on taking AI rules to a board or committee.

**Template released:** Disclosure wording for your website and documents, and a one-page board paper outline for adopting AI rules.

**Member action:** Take your adapted one-page rule sheet to your team or board and get a decision: adopted, adopted with changes, or a date for when it will be decided.

**Facilitator note:** Remind members that the templates are starting points, not legal advice. Mention the AI Safe-Use Policy Sprint once, for boards that want a full policy.

## Week 8: Hand it over

**Module:** 4. Navigate (part two): making it last.

**Weekly prompt:** "Show your work. Post your workflow canvas with three lines: what it does, what you timed, and the catch. Rough is fine."

**Live session:** None this week. Recorded walkthrough, about 10 minutes: handing a workflow to a colleague, and choosing the next one.

**Template released:** Handover checklist and a 90-day plan for the next workflow.

**Member action:** Have a colleague run your workflow from the written canvas without your help. Fix whatever confused them. Then choose your second workflow.

**Facilitator note:** Second check on the promise. Record how many active members have a workflow in use and a set of rules adopted. Ask three members whether they would share their experience publicly, with written consent. No one is quoted without it.

## What Kris tracks each week

- Members who answered the weekly prompt.
- Attendance at live sessions and views of recordings.
- Members with a chosen workflow (week 1), a canvas (week 4), a named reviewer (week 6), adopted rules (week 7) and a handover (week 8).
- 90-day retention against the 75% target, and monthly churn against the under 5% target.

## Standing notes

- Every demonstration uses published or fictional material. Never a real member's information, from any organisation.
- No SCN2A Australia build or material is shown as an example without the board's consent.
- Say where AI was used to produce member materials.
- Nothing in the community is medical, legal or privacy advice. Rare Intelligence is not affiliated with SCN2A Australia, Rare Diseases NSW or any public body.
