# AI for rare disease organisations: start safely, build one workflow

**Length:** 4 hours, including a 20-minute break. Example timing 9:00 am to 1:00 pm.
**Group size:** up to 15.
**Who it is for:** people who run or work in rare disease organisations and health charities. No technical knowledge needed.

## What participants leave with

1. A one-page set of AI rules for their organisation, in draft, ready to take to their team.
2. One workflow built and tested on their own (non-identifying) material.
3. A checking routine for AI output.
4. A 30-day plan: one action, one owner, one date.

## What it is not

Not medical advice, not legal advice, and not a product demonstration. No real patient or family information is used at any point.

## Before the day

**Two weeks out: email participants.**

- Bring a laptop and a login to one general AI assistant, such as Claude or ChatGPT. A free account is enough for the exercises. [Kris: confirm this still holds for the tools you demonstrate.]
- Choose one task you would like help with. Bring one piece of your own material for it that contains no personal information: a past grant section, a published newsletter, a public research paper, or meeting notes with names and anything sensitive removed.
- Do not bring member lists, case notes or anything that identifies a patient, family, donor or staff member.
- Optional: take the AI readiness quiz and note your score.

**In-house groups:** ask the organiser for a short call first. Find out which tools are already in use, whether a policy exists and what the leadership most wants from the day. Run the conflict check before accepting.

**Facilitator preparation.**

- Prepare one fully fictional practice pack for anyone who arrives without material: a public-domain style paper abstract you wrote yourself, a made-up set of meeting notes and a sample newsletter. Label every page "fictional example".
- Test every demonstration prompt the day before in the tools you will show. Tools change; do not assume last month's behaviour.
- Print materials (list below).

## Facilitator notes

**The 15 sorting cards.** A published research paper. Your latest public newsletter. A member's email describing their child's seizures. A spreadsheet of donors. A draft strategic plan. Survey free-text answers with names removed. The same answers grouped into themes with small groups combined. Board minutes. A photo from a family day. A grant application with staff salaries. A grant application with salaries removed. A confidential briefing from a company partner. A public consultation paper. Notes from a staff performance conversation. A parent's story that the parent has already published on your website.

Suggested answers: green for the published paper, public newsletter, consultation paper and the already-published story (for uses the consent covers). Amber for the draft plan, grouped themes and the grant application with salaries removed. Red for the email, donor spreadsheet, photo, salaries, company briefing, performance notes and, unless the board has decided otherwise, board minutes. The names-removed survey answers are the discussion card: red until the de-identification test is passed.

**Standing rules for the facilitator.**

- Never demonstrate with real member information, from any organisation.
- Do not name or discuss specific medicines or products.
- Do not present time-saved figures. Ask participants to time their own task.
- Stay tool-neutral. Say "an AI assistant such as Claude or ChatGPT", and do not make claims about a tool's features you have not checked that week.
- Do not refer to any public appointment or committee role.
- If a participant describes a possible privacy incident, thank them, keep the detail out of the room and offer to talk afterwards.
- If sponsored, state the sponsor and the terms in the welcome: unrestricted support, no say over content or participants, no individual data.

**If things go wrong.** Wifi down: run the sorting exercise and rule sheet first, and demonstrate from a phone hotspot. A tool is unavailable: switch to the second tool. Someone has no material: hand them a fictional pack.

**Accessibility.** Send slides in advance on request, use captions online, keep type large and allow carers to step out and back in without comment.
