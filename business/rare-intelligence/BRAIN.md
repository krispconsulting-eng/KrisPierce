# Business brain: Rare Intelligence

Line 2 of 3. Status: site built, model undecided. Brand: Deep Signal (locked). "Rare by nature. Intelligent by design."

## 1. The business in one paragraph

Rare disease organisations are run by a handful of people doing the work of thirty. Most of them are already using AI, quietly and without rules. Rare Intelligence helps the people who run those organisations put AI to work safely: a membership where they build real workflows alongside peers, short fixed-price engagements that leave a policy and a working system behind, and courses for their teams. It is led by someone who has run one of these organisations and knows what the work is.

## 3. Why now

- 67% of Australian not-for-profits use generative AI; 14% have a policy or guideline for it (Infoxchange 2025, via PwC, May 2026).
- In a 2026 survey of rare disease patients and caregivers, 54.8% had used generative AI for their condition and 78.3% had not discussed what it told them with a clinician. Patient organisations are being asked about AI by their communities and have no position.
- General AI training for not-for-profits is free or cheap (Gradient Institute, government-funded Infoxchange). Nobody found offers it for rare disease organisations, with their privacy, evidence and industry-relationship questions.
- No AI programme was found from Rare Voices Australia, EURORDIS, Global Genes or NORD.

Sources in `../research/01-ai-and-consulting-market.md`.

## 6. Strategy

**Position.** AI for the people who run rare disease organisations, from someone who has run one. Anti-hype by design: name the tool, the workflow and the time saved.

**How it wins.** Specificity. A generic course teaches prompting. Rare Intelligence teaches how to draft a consumer submission with AI without inventing evidence, how to summarise a trial for families, how to handle a member's story safely, and what to tell a sponsor about your AI use.

**Growth without more days.** Membership revenue repeats. Sponsored places bring in ten members with one conversation. Courses sell while Kris sleeps. Services stay capped at about 30 days a year and exist mainly to produce case material and members.

**Honest risk.** A $97 membership for small charities will be slow. Fifty members in year one is a reasonable base case; it took most comparable communities longer. The founding rate, the sponsored places and annual billing are the three levers. If membership is under 25 by 30 June 2027, drop the community to a lighter format and lead with services and courses.

**Twelve-month targets (base case).** 50 members by December 2027, 8 consults, 4 policy sprints, 2 intensives, 2 workshops, 1 sponsored cohort: about $79,000, with the sponsored cohort counted inside membership.
